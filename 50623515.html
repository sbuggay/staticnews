To add a bit about the confusing (and borderline nonsensical IMO) state of affairs. The FAA contends that it regulates approximately anything that flys regardless of height. SCOTUS has affirmed this above the (potential?) treeline IIUC (a remarkably vague boundary). Below that at some point states rights must prevail over federal interests but (unless something has changed) it's not at all clear how the details will shake out. The FAA continues to pretend that this isn't the case as they draft up ever more rules and no one in their right mind wants to go to court with them so here we are.

That's a wonderful trick to be able to perform, and I'm pleased to hear that you had good results with it.

But, I mean: I don't gather car parts out of junk yards often, but when I do, then... I look at the website for their inventory, call them to verify (or if I'm desperate or the website doesn't exist), and then I just show up and walk up to the counter and talk to a real person.

Sometimes, the answer is "Third row, on the left, about a quarter of the way out on the first chunk."

More often, someone goes out with me and directs me towards what I'm looking for.

Or at least, that's what happens when I pull the parts myself.

When they pull the parts on their own, sometimes I don't go into the yard at all -- sometimes, those parts are already harvested, indexed and stored indoors, and waiting for someone like me to show up and buy them.

And other times, I go out there with some grizzled dude whose mentality is "You just want the hub and brake parts for the left-rear? If I just torch off that whole section, would that be OK with you?"

Whatever the case, they've always got a good idea of what they've got -- and they want to help me buy it from them.

And sometimes, well beyond that: When I needed a door handle for a GMC Safari (because it's not a question of if a zinc GM door handle will fail, but when) and wanted to buy a used example, I popped into a yard where I'd seen some of those vans piled up out back and asked for one. They said didn't have any, and then they sold me a brand new one for a perfectly-reasonable price. Bulk-packed, in a plain bag from a cardboard box filled with others, and less than $20.

"These are really popular," he said as he handed it over the counter, "so we keep new ones in stock."
