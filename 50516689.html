Sometimes syntactic duplication can give you a hint to underlying semantic duplication. But it's no more than a hint.

DRY doesn't mean don't repeat a similar looking block of code, it means try to structure your code so that each element of the design has a single representation.

There's no shortcut to good design where no thinking is required, much as a lot of people seem to wish there were.

Abstractions on the other hand introduce the risk of 'what random thing am I going to break by changing this?' bugs which are typically much harder and more time consuming to find and fix. Further, the risk is recursive, as the fix might break yet another thing.
