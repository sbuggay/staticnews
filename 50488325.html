brudgers|parent|prev|next|

Shallower algorithms have fewer overtones and the overtones tend to be harmonic.

Deeper algorithms have more overtones and often those overtones are enharmonic.

But also how you tune and mix plays a role…a shallow algorithm witH carriers tuned one way will sound consonant and with a different tuning will sound dissonant.

Or to put it another way, FM is awesome for sound design but like everything in music it is hard to learn quickly.

I imagine you are vaguely familiar with subtractive synthesis; oscillators, filters, envelopes, modulation. It wouldn't hurt, although the building blocks are laid out differently in an FM synth.

Anyway, I found these hands-on demos on the DX1 to be some of the most "aha" moments I've had trying to understand how FM patches are made.

TLDR, an FM "operator" is an oscillator. A carrier is the oscillator you hear, its modulator is an oscillator that wiggles the carrier and brings out different harmonics. You can stack more modulators and you can have multiple carriers going in parallel, the various combinations of operators available in a synth being the different algorithms.

My mental model is : each carrier is a sound (a harmonic). I can stack the sounds by chosing an algorithm with multiple carriers. So I start with one carrier and sculpt it by chosing frequency and envelope. This is the time I also activate it's modulators and try to tweak them to make the sound better. Modulators have very complex effects on the sound and I don't try to have a mental model of what they do, just empirical outlines. The next step is to add another carrier to the sound and continue tweaking the current and all the previous carriers.
