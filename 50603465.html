It's great that it has such a lineage and is still around, but according to its history it was a bbs / netnews login host. World.std.com was providing internet access in 89 or 90 by log in, and of course many universities by then had hordes of people dialing up, logging in, and using the net; A similar BBS->net hybrid was the Well.

Pobably The Little Garden (later TLGNet) in 1990 was the first ISP in the sense it's used today: there were no user hosts that you logged into and then ran programs; it literally only provided IP service and you had to have a router to connect.

We gave instructions on how to make a router with KA9Q and and an old PC, to those who needed it.

Fun trivia: the first person who managed TLG full time (before that it was just a spinoff of Cygnus, with its first POP my spare bedroom) was Tom Jennings, the author of FIDONET one of the Ur-BBS systems, and surely the biggest.

There are a lot of folks from that time on HN.

PS: the name The Little Garden came from my favorite restaurant back then, a famous hacker hangout where folks from PARC, Stanford, SRI, Sun etc would hang out. Gosper was a regular.

Today it means that.

In the 80's and very early 90's, there were hundreds of national, regional, and local computer portals. Many of them offered a subset of internet features. Some had e-mail gateways, others usenet, or gopher, or uucp, or some combination. They provided internet services. Sometimes direct through the protocol, and sometimes behind their own user interface.

A raw pipe to the net supporting all protocols, as we expect it today, would come later.
