I read At the Mountains of Madness in high school from a battered paperback I borrowed from my uncle, and the fossil angle never clicked for me until now. I always thought Lovecraft was inventing the whole thing. Knowing he was riffing on real finds from his own time makes the story feel less like pure invention and more like a guy reading the news and getting carried away, which I like more, honestly.

You see a lot of this in older science fiction. They were writing "science fiction", but we read it as "science fiction".

There's tons of things like ESP or pheromones or giant red suns which a contemporary reader, who's not very familiar with the history of ideas or science, will read as colorful fantasy, and yet the original author was giving a speculation, or believed it was simple fact (https://gwern.net/dune-genetics#scientifiction).
