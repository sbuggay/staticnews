What is the difference between a system, a dynamic system and a dynamical system?

OgsyedIE|root|parent|next|

A system, at least within the foundations of physics, is any open set considered worthwhile to study.

A dynamic system is a system considered in the context of some theory of physics that evolve in time, such as classical dynamics or electrodynamics.

A dynamical system is a submanifold of a jet bundle over the product space of some open set and a time manifold which is almost always R with the most common exception being S^1, typically written as J^1(R×M), considered with some regularity conditions that permit you to do analysis over any contour lines of your choosing in the dynamical system. Essentially, it's a rigorous way to study what happens when you mix arbitrary differential equations and arbitrary differentiable manifolds, such as in the phase spaces of any random IRL shit.

A "dynamical system" is mostly just a shared abstraction that's useful for theorizing on the behaviour of systems and their behaviour over time -- especially a common set of phenomena like chaos, etc.

System is too general and underspecified and dynamic system is unclearly specified because you done know whether it readily exposes the interfaces/frameworks that someone would use to theorize about dynamical systems.

So dynamic means nothing but the 'al' means interface+framework+theorize ?

OgsyedIE|root|parent|next|

The addition of the Latin-originating -al suffix to the Greek word dynamic was deliberately chosen in 1927 when dynamical systems where first widely accepted by mathematicians to be a useful way of framing things in order to specifically separate it from the dozen things in physics that already used the name dynamics, since the mathematicians looking to axiomatise the things we now call dynamical systems wanted to get physicists to stay away from it and let the math community keep it for themselves.

Back at that point in history there was a much fiercer rivalry between the two fields.
