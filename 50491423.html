joshka|parent|next|

Yep,and I want to do that too :)

I've written about it before, but in brief the mountain of standards is worth ripping out wholesale and doing write as a semantic protocol. It's needed to solve several things that work really poorly in terminals because their unit of compatibility is cells rather than a higher primitive. It's hard to bake something on top that works well.

The need is most strong in Accessibility, Text wrapping, Scrollback handling (i.e. making the scrollback editable and not dead text), and various other bits that make writing highly functional TUIs. Coding agents tend to find all those edge cases.

This often gets push back that it's easy, to which I can point nay sayers at the various many months long issues on things in Codex, Gemini, Claude etc. on flickering, scrolling, key handline, IME, ... doing anything moderately complex involves modeling a terminal in a terminal app effectively.

Ghostty and Rex sort of do the opposite bit. If you think of it like a stack, they write a new layer underneath that transports the cells on a better multiplexer layer 0. I think a new semantic layer 2 is an approach that would be neat to work with.

One benefit worth calling out: the box-drawing fallback is worse than it looks for CJK users. U+2500-257F and the half/full block elements are East Asian "Ambiguous" in UAX #11, so in a terminal configured to render ambiguous as double width (xterm's cjkWidth, iTerm2's checkbox) every line glyph takes two cells and the whole frame lands in the wrong place. Cell borders aren't text, so that failure class just disappears.

The part I'd want specced early: borders are per-cell, but cells aren't per-grapheme. A CJK char owns two cells plus a continuation placeholder, and combining marks or ZWJ emoji sequences own zero-or-more depending on whose wcwidth table you ask, so app and terminal routinely disagree across Unicode versions. Today that shows up as a vertical bar one column off; with cell borders it becomes a line that stops short of the corner. Does the border attach to the cell or to the grapheme cluster?
