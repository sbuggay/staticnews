Having built a pulsed vibration wristband for someone with Parkinson's, just a few notes. For Parkinson's patients:

  - there's a small-ish window in the disease progression where pulsed vibration is able to help a person regain some intentional motor control
  - the device doesn't stop the tremors
  - the pulsed vibration does let the brain's control signal come through stronger, so drawing/typing/moving the mouse is more intentional.  
  - this is no more effective than the results of levidopa/carbidopa treatment, and the levidopa/carbidopa typically calms the overall tremors
  - as Parkinson's progresses, these devices become useless, and if the patient qualifies for deep brain stimulation, it's worth looking into (regain years of useful life if you're lucky).
I haven't tested the device on people with essential tremor, so I don't have anything smart to say there.

You can make a very unsophisticated version of this without a lot of hardware:

  - small microcontroller with 3v outputs (XIAO's are perfect)
  - 3v sealed eccentric mass motors (aliexpress $1/ea)
  - elastic wristband
  - usb battery
  - software to pulse the motors for about 200 miliseconds for an on off sequence of about 8 times, then rest for about a second and repeat.
Hopefully these folks will price it affordably.

Side note: the idea was published in 2016-2017 in a few promotional videos from Microsoft research: https://www.windowscentral.com/microsofts-emma-watch-and-pro...

Well, one adjustment is that it looks like any high temperature process resulting in a maillard-type reaction can elevate these. So I suppose roasted vegetables are going to have elevated levels as well. Darker crusty breads more than lighter breads, etc. Smoking and coffee are higher risk factors than food, although idk if that's accounting for rate/volume of consumption.

A (very) quick look at this says that the compounds are doing the damage, but the real problem is that some people don't metabolize them as quickly as others, and they build up. These are neurotoxic at higher concentrations and cross the blood-brain barrier, so if you aren't clearing them out then I guess you're exposing your cerebellum to more of them for a longer duration.

Studies suggest that people with ET have higher levels of these beta-carbolines in their blood than control patients, in some cases (like familial tremor) even if their diet and lifestyle doesn't look it should be risk.

First, “fda cleared” means your device is “substantially equivalent” to another cleared or granted device. mandevil|parent|next|

MIT uses a weird class numbering system. Instead of CS101, they call it 6.0002.

The first digit is the school that offers it, then after the decimal point they have their number of classes. (EECS is unusual in that it has four digits after the decimal point, most every other course has just three.) In general, classes that are required start with 001 and increment up, such that 001 is the intro course, 002 follows from that, etc. Then electives start around 015 or so.

Course 1 is Civil Engineering, Course 2 is Mechanical engineering, Course 3 is materials science, etc. 6 is Electrical and Computer engineering.

So 2.009 is a mechanical engineering course, in this case one for students towards the end of their time. It's called Product Engineering Processes, where you create a new product from beginning to production.
