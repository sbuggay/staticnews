chilmers|root|parent|next|

Even in societies where social care is relatively well-funded, instances of neglect, abuse and substandard care are rife. This is not a problem that can be solved by throwing more money at human carers.

Providing full-time care for seniors is incredibly demanding, both physically and mentally. There is a limited pool of people with the requisite mindset and aptitude to do it well. Better renumeration, while certainly deserved by those people, won't automatically increase the size of this pool, it may simply attract more ill-suited people to the profession.

And even with the most compassionate and skilled carers, needing to rely on other human beings for your physical needs results in a loss of dignity and independence. It also creates a power imbalance that will always make abuse or neglect a possibility.

Done right, automated care offers the possibility to provide high-quality physical care that is more convenient, private and dignified for the recipient than needing everything to be done by a human. It also allows for that pool of skilled human carers to focus on higher-value support without being spending the majority of the time addressing basic physical needs.

There is no need to hedge against accidentally coming off as luddite. We could all use a refresher of what it actually was, as compared to how it tends to be taught.

Luddism is often mistaken for irrational or uneducated “progress bad”, “screw technology”, “destroy machines”. In reality[0], many of them actually operated those machines (there is no dichotomy between “use technology” and “be a luddite”).
