0x20cowboy|root|parent|prev|next|

I played around with this for a while, and I don't think it's possible without making training / inference extremely slow - if it's possible at all. The issue I think is doing back propagation against the weights and trying to say "X training data had this much influence on this gradient run" can't be stored in (or inferred from) the weights. So it'd have to be stored externally in some kind of log which would be difficult to sort out because early input would have a higher movement on the weights than later input and that movement amount isn't exactly related to how much it helped - also batching.

Then from there if you wanted to use it at inference time it gets harder because you have to know which part of the network was used the most to make that output, and then somehow look that up in some table somewhere.

I am not a smart person so there might be better ways to do that, but from my gutter experiments I don't think it would work with how current NN work. At least not built into the weights, but I've been playing with an external thing that hopefully help with attribution.

I also got confused by some parts, but some parts made some sense to me:

> Imagine a curiosity that aims less to know and more to make connections, build constellations, find links, and follow threads, functioning within a webbed network of relations between knowers, methods of knowing, and knowledges.

I understood this as thinking of knowledge as including what it builds upon e.g Newton's law of gravity worked because it explained Kepler's laws, and Kepler's laws worked because they explained Tycho's data, and Tycho was able to collect his data because of the invention of clear glass lenses, and so on.

> As such, knowledge is not properly propositional but instead procedural; it is less concerned with knowing what than with knowing how. And its wisdom lies in “sharing” more than “stating.”

It makes sense to make that sharing the how is more important than sharing the what, that is the concept that underlies replication of the results of scientific experiments.

I also thought it was an interesting linguistic fact that curiosity used to refer to an attribute of a busybody, but that does not seem to help the points above because language changes, e.g terrific changing from a negative to a positive word.

Edit: After looking it up, I have found out that Tycho didn't actually use a telescope, he used a sextant, and the higher quality of his data was because he made his own improvements to the design of the sextant.
