fwn|root|parent|next|

There is this idea that the primary threat to privacy from cloud email providers is third parties reading emails during transit/in real time.

Another idea is to compare cloud email providers with much more closed systems, like Signal, and conclude that, since recipient inboxes can never be considered secure, encrypting cloud email storage is moot.

However, (supervillains aside) in-transit surveillance and other people's inboxes are edge cases at best.

There is a much more trivial danger: criminals or law enforcement gaining access to specifically your entire inbox of the last 20 years and letting a crooked AI loose on it.

After that, the threat actor will have your movement profile from your flight and Uber receipts. They will have an overview of every account you have and probably your full payment history. ...all since middle school.

To reduce that risk on the provider side, your cloud provider needs to be able to claim truthfully to be unable to read the data; hence, encryption.

Encryption for e-mail serves a purpose, despite what others are saying: When Kagi gets compromised, an attacker does not automatically get access to your unencrypted inbox. So while it’s possible to long term compromise them (by injecting malware into their systems), it adds enough of buffer that there is a point to it.
