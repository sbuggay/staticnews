Proton can’t change the fundamental nature of IMAP or SMTP (and still be an “email” service. Their MTA must be able to read certain fields in the plain text email header; this doesn’t even change with PGP encrypted mail. Most MTAs use TLS for transport security. That prevents eavesdropping in transit. If you want maximal privacy from Proton, then you have the option of using PGP which introduces very high bars to working with others over email- you have to exchange keys out of band in a secure fashion, and then the other party has to either use a PGP-enabled mailer or has to know hotel to encrypt and decrypt.

Proton claims that their MTAs store the messages encrypted with a key for which they don’t possess the decryption key. This doesn’t seem like an unreasonable claim; this is a very well established pattern.
