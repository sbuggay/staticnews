The native JS semantics are UCS-2. Saying that it's UTF-16 is misleading and confuses charset, encoding and browser APIs.

Ladybird is probably implementing support properly but it's annoying that they keep spreading the confusion in their article.


dzaima|root|parent|

It's not clearly one or the other, really. It's UCS-2-y by `str.length` or `str[i]`, but UTF-16-y by `str.codePointAt(i)` or by iteration (`[...str]` or `for (x of str)`). Generally though JS's strings are just a list of 16-bit values, being intrinsically neither UCS-2 nor UTF-16.
