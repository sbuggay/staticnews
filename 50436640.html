SVMs in big data were already well liked back in 2010 as they could be scaled via map-reduce.

An issue in industry was that companies would not allow training on their data across customers. Understandably so, as SVM dimensions are readily interpretable given some thought. General purpose datasets were ill-suited too depending on the task.

So you'd start from scratch every time, annotate features like crazy, and often didn't have enough training data overall.

LLMs "solved" that by just ignoring the previous rules and training on everything, which lets the necessary world knowledge seep back in.

I'm sure we'll see many combinations of LLMs and regular machine intelligence in the near future.

nlpnerd|prev|next|

Founder of Seldon AI (www.seldon-ai.com) here. We are working on dev tools to help developers with this kind of refactoring and continual production work.

Basically there is a whole host of tarpit usage of LLMs in application backends today. This is simply another case of LLMs enabling people to build what they cannot productionize or maintain.

Today developers without any AI/ML skillset can prototype a fancy new feature with LLMs, what used to require collecting and curating data to train a pipeline of ML models then serving them in production.

The problem comes when it's time to take the new feature into production. That $20 cost with prototype/beta workload is now $30k per month in LLM costs, and those hallucinations that never happened on their 200 test samples is happening 600 times per day.

They are then stuck, because they lack the knowledge and budget to build and run the production ML pipeline properly, the infra stack necessary to handle production workload. Management also doesn't understand why they have to invest in rebuilding something that "seems to work" with "outdated methods".

These system one or decision models are a step up from LLMs when it comes to this usage. Problem is that they are still difficult to work with (limited in expression power) for many application logic. Despite the initial popularity, I hear about a lot of frictions refactoring LLM based backend with them, and multiple cases of production rollbacks to LLMs already.

I think system one models is a sensible paradigm, and we are on the precipice of something great, but there is a lot more work needed above the model layer to make this work.
