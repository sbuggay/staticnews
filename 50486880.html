Serious whisky collector (and drinker) here...

You don't need a fancy glass. Your sense of smell makes a huge difference when it comes to food and drink, as anybody with a nasty cold can tell you. You want something that keeps it from diffusing into the air so anything with a wide mouth makes it harder to appreciate.

So: a cognac snifter; sherry glass; a glencairn, which was ""made for whisky""; etc.

But of course it's down to taste.

If the aroma is too heady? Blow air into glass and then inhale the aroma. The burn is gone (important if you're drinking 62% rocket fuel single cask whisky) and you get a more dilute smell.

No need for all this pretension; this glass is that.

regentbowerbird|root|parent|next|

Old-fashioned glasses have thick walls and fat bottoms because that is the style for them. "Cut glass" decoration requires some thickness.

Norlan is double-walled in order to keep let the drinker sip their whisky without it warming up. A lot of people drink their whisky cold, both because it softens the attack and also because it changes the taste a bit, and whisky drinking is all about drinking the same thing over and over again and discovering new things each time.

As a side note Norlan glasses are borosilicate and therefore perfectly appropriate for hot beverages. I suspect they might actually be some of the best tea tasting glasses available although I haven't tried it out yet.
