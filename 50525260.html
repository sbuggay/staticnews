A bit of context that didn't make it into the writeup:

The 819LMB is a HomeKit based device, meaning by HomeKit spec it is supposed to function 100% locally on your LAN with zero internet access. (most connect it to home assistant)

In their infinite wisdom, Chamberlain decided to cripple it anyway: if the garage door opener cannot reach their cloud, a firmware watchdog triggers a hard reboot every 30 minutes, taking down local HomeKit control along with it. Additionally, if you don't block the internet the right way, it reboots every 10 minutes in addition to the 30 minute reboot.

doodlebugging|root|parent|next|

Like you, I also had no idea that one of the springs could shatter suddenly. There is a lot of stored energy in that spring steel when the garage door is closed. My doors are older doors that use a spring configuration that is more dangerous than the modern doors. Modern garage doors use torsion springs I believe and the springs used are wound/unwound around a durable rod so that if or when the spring fails, large parts are constrained from flying off in random directions. The old style spring setup had no means of preventing large chunks of spring steel from exploding into the garage and destroying things. If your garage doors use the old configuration you can make them safer by passing a cable through the center of the spring and firmly anchoring it at each end. That will prevent most of the spring from becoming airborne just as the torsion bar does in modern garage doors. My old doors had zero safety cables so I added a safety cable to each door when I replaced the springs that failed.

Springs do fail. They have a limited number of cycles that they are rated to deliver. Here is a useful link to help understand what type of spring that your doors use and how durable that it is.

https://www.precisiondoor.net/torsion-and-extension-springs

This was definitely not anywhere on my list of things to consider when we bought the house more than 20 years ago. I will be replacing the springs with a torsion spring system at some point in the future. Right now they have safety cables installed.
