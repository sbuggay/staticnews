hazrmard|prev|next|

JEPA is very similar to Model Predictive Control. Here, you swap the model of the system with black-box simulator (a neural net), apply some version of dynamic programming / Bellman equation to find the optimal action over planning horizon.

In the planning stage, by factoring out the optimization step from the rollout step, you can free the model to focus on learning the dynamics in latent space. (The other case, where the rollout+optimization happen in the learning loop, just becomes model-free RL.)

My background straddles classical & data-driven controls. This was the first similarity that popped out to me when I first started learning about world models.

Is JEPA just MPC but where the "M" is a latent-space model?

I think this is why it has a lot of potential. I'm also controls background, so I'm going to just throw stuff at the wall.

I'm thinking it's both M and P. M for the reasons you've mentioned. Then there is P where a forward horizon prediction of both plant and the world.

I think JEPA also touches on the perception tasks: What to do with sensor data? Classical control objectives are quite simple, drive the state to stable, follow a trajectory, stay safe. It's very deterministic: Sensor data is used to determine state. State is used to update dynamics, dynamics is used for the forward horizon.

So. MPC works for things like self-driving because the car model is fairly knowable and world model is predictable (rules of the road, knowable dynamics of other cars).

JEPA seems like a fusion of control, sensing, world model into an embedding space where the learning that occurs is a model free approach to how these are all related to each other.

LarsDu88|root|parent|next|

JEPA can be applied to text as well in the sense you can use it to build text embedding models, freeze your JEPA text embedder, then train a decoder over the top of that.

OF course your decoder model could end up doing a lot of the heavy lifting if your decoder loss was based on text reconstruction methods (which almost all LLMs use for eval purposes)

For certain tasks, you do need pixel/token level reconstruction fidelity to do a lot of things. Like image generation or text generation.

For other tasks, having a coherent representation of the world that can be computed quickly and intuitively is more salient. I suspect the human mind works more like JEPA than like an autoregressive LLM. Human children train on far less data to achieve extreme fidelity on a lot of tasks generative reconstruction loss based AI/ML techniques struggle with.
