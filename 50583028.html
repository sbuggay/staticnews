This is largely in line with Tim Galwey's "Inner Game" books [0] specifically and Zen in general.

After reading many of Gallwey's books and also Zen and the Art of Archery [1], I would summarize it all down into one sentence:

Western coaching focuses on telling your body what to do, Zen coaching focuses on observing what your body is doing.

e.g. this story from the Inner Game of Skiing [2] summarizes it well:

"I didn't tell people how to stand, shift their weight etc. I just told them:

- observe the angle of your skis to the ground

- flat on the ground is 1

- as far over as you can tilt is 10

- just feel what angle you are at as your are moving down the hill.

A young lady then came up to ask me 'So what is the correct angle??'. I told her 'There is no correct angle, just observe what angle your skis are.' "

If you prefer a video example, this youtube video of Gallwey teaching people hit play tennis for the first time is great [3] (note: it's very low video quality but excellent at showing his techniques).

Over 20 years ago I quit smoking after being a pack and a half smoker since high school. I didn’t torture myself by trying to quit, by cutting down or using some aid like nicotine alternatives.

Instead, I simply ran through a simple mental exercise every time I started a cigarette. I imagined and felt the few things that I really didn’t like about smoking - the look and smell of an old full ashtray, the taste of a stale cigarette, the gagging feeling of a first daily cigarette one sometimes experiences, the feeling of smoke in my eyes etc. Every cigarette every time, and after that I would just carry on with my cigarette. I didn’t try to give up smoking.

After a few weeks I realized I was often stubbing out the cigarettes early, not through a conscience decision! A few weeks later I just … stopped wanting a cigarette, and haven’t wanted one since.

Today, for the first time I hear of Allen Carr’s technique. It’s not the same, but related and I’m amazed I never heard of it! Both it seems result in changing one’s core beliefs?

One note of warning: I think this technique could be really dangerous if applied to things like overeating etc.

Another important point I think is that I was in an environment conducive to stopping smoking - my wife hated it. If your in an environment where smoking is rewarded, like for example smoking with a buddy, or regular pub sessions, it surely would be less effective.
