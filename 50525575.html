You might have heard about sRGB (the commonly used colorspace) not being linear, and that to get halfway between black and white (linear RGB of 50%), you get an sRGB value of around 74%.

But there is one place where sRGB is truly linear, and that is signal voltage for a CRT display. 50% of sRGB truly means 50% of signal voltage (between the minimum and maximum voltages).

rasz|prev|next|

In numbers:

VGA default gamma y = 1 aka linear

3DFX default gamma y = 1.5

You might, like I did, think wtf is he talking about, default is linear and 3dfx bends it. But CRT monitors y = 2.2! When added up (multiply exponents) 3dfx is closer to linear output than ordinary VGA. You would still have to set 3dfx to 2.2 to get perfect linear output.

TLDR: Carmack is partially right. As he says even game developers were clueless. Nobody had to change their workflows. You could call glide grGammaCorrectionValue or even easier set DOS environment variable SST_GAMMA to 1 to get Exact same color mapping as ordinary VGA.

Why did 3dfx boost that? deffo not to get linear output, if they wanted linear they would set 2.2. Probably a Color twist on loudness war, brighter better, or maybe felt bad half the bits would get wasted (crushed into near blacks) in 16bit rendering.
