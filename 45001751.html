Nobody thinks Greenland is the most important place in the World - although as as a relevant political sidepoint, the US political administration is very clear they would like to take control of it for "reasons", so it clearly has something going for it.

However, ask yourself why the mercator projection was so popular for so long, and why resistance to other projections took so long to counter.

Maps are inherently political and about power. The lines drawn on a map indicate the concrete physical extent of abstract non-physical forces such as power, authority, and jurisdiction of law.

When groups of people went about the World for centuries (arguably millennia), stealing, torturing, kidnapping, enslaving, raping and murdering people to create new lines on maps of their authority and law, it's fair to say where those lines are drawn is important, and when those same people decide to teach these events in a very sanitised way using maps that make their own countries look far bigger than they are, it's reasonable to ask what is going on.

But we can put that aside for the snowflakes who get upset about discussion of historical events related to European and American empire building (and I say this as a white, middle-aged, British man).

In plain terms: it's sometimes useful to have an accurately sized map if you want to compare by eye the size of say the Falklands (or Malvinas, if you prefer), with Malta or Jamaica, and not get a false sense of relative size just because of how somebody decided to draw the map without caring about accurate sizing. This is useful in a wide range of educational settings relating to geography (a physical, non-political version is available for download), history, politics (versions with Palestine and Israel labelled or not labelled are available), and even economics.

It's just a fact that maps that are accurate to land-mass size like this are more useful for some purposes than those that aren't, and doing so for a large ball onto a 2D surface is hard, and a worthwhile pursuit for lots of end use cases, for reasons that are well documented in a variety of forms to suit your taste if you're curious.

The "kinds of people to complain about" the mercator projection therefore can be people who just want a more accurately sized map. What's your problem with them? Why are you so against them wanting another map projection? They aren't taking your preferred inaccurately sized map away, they're just creating a new one fit for their purposes, and you're the one complaining about it. As such, I actually think you're the one who needs to explain your thinking here. It's your thinking that I would argue is bizarre.

Perhaps you also missed the fact that different meridian options are available well away from the Greenwich meridian, and there's even a "South to the top" projection available. Perhaps you think those people who would appreciate such a map for purposes you can't imagine, aren't entitled to it, even though them having that costs you exactly nothing? It's an odd notion, far odder than the notion of wanting such things for all sorts of reasons.

PaulRobinson|parent|next|

There is literally a huge and prominent link entitled "Equal Earth Projection" in the middle, at the top, that when clicked take you to a description of what the intent of the projection was:

    It was created to provide a visually pleasing alternative to the Gall-Peters projection, which some schools and socially concerned groups have adopted out of concern for fairness. Their priority is to show developing countries in the tropics and developed countries in the north with correctly proportioned sizes.

    In addition to being rigorously equal-area throughout, other Equal Earth projection features include:

    •  An overall shape similar to that of the Robinson projection. (The Robinson, although popular and pleasing to the eye, is not equal-area as is the Equal Earth projection).

    •  The curved sides of the projection suggest the spherical form of Earth.

    •  Straight parallels that make it easier to compare how far north or south places are from the equator.
Perhaps that makes it clearer for you.
