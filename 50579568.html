An interesting thing about the Riemann hypothesis is that there are a lot of other conjectures that have no obvious connection to it but that turn out to be equivalent. Equivalent in this context means that if any of them are true then all of them are.

Here is one that is easy to understand with a high school level of math:

Let H(n) = 1 + 1/2 + 1/3 + ... + 1/n, where n is a positive integer.

Let D(n) = the sum of the positive integers that divide a positive integer n. For example D(12) = 1 + 2 + 3 + 4 + 6 + 12 = 28.

Conjecture: For n > 1, we have D(n) < H(n) + exp(H(n)) log(H(n)).

With so many different conjectures turning out to be equivalent, I'll not be surprised if it turns out that there are also multiple different ways to prove it.
