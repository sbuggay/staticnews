They don't really show it here, but supposedly a Cerebras' chip is sub-divided into a 2D mesh of what they call PEs (cores). So data flow generally flows in two axis, and maintains consistent timings to its 4 neighboring cores. They show the layers flowing from left-to-right being streamed by PEs, but I wonder how true that ends up being: maybe the communications are easier to stream via diagonals, or some other shape that has better topology for data flow.

Now, I assume that some parts of what we call a core can be moved around more freely while maintaining whatever power/heating/timing requirements we have, so perhaps it's easier to 'force' a square by moving those loose parts around. bubblethink|parent|prev|next|

The reason 2D grids work well is because you can split tensor axes along whatever dim you want across PEs (batch, sequence, hidden, etc.). And your per PE code is relatively simple. It's of the type : get data from neighbor, compute my subtensor, send to neighbor. And you can implement broadcasts and reductions along any axis.

bubblethink|parent|next|

It's describing a different type of parallelism - pipeline parallelism. The network is laid out spatially on the wafer in CB's case. So weights are stationary and activations flow. A GPU is going to process the network one layer at a time. GPUs also use other types of parallelism (tensor and expert mainly in inference). Pipeline parallelism doesn't make much sense on a GPU for inference.
