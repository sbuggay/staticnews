Former tailscalar here and now someone managing tailscale at an org.

Having done my own review of the vulns and the fixes as they are landing in github, its pretty clear to me that this only affects situations where you use ACLs to permit ssh into a node (via tailscale ssh, not via opensshd), but dont give a path to sudoers/root.

Not trying to play down the vuln (its important), but its not a free-for-all RCE that some ppl are acting like it is.

> Obviously tailscale has some great devs, security is complex.

Quick reminder that in recent months Tailscale has had issues with age-old classic vulnerabilities such as input validation.

The problem with Tailscale is twofold.

First their attack surface is too big. They continuously shoehorn new features into the product.

Second they don't appear to do regular deep-dive code audits and instead they appear to rely mostly on the goodwill of third-parties to poke at select areas of their code as and when those third-parties feel like it. Hence simple vulnerabilities such as input validation keep on popping up.

Fancy SOC2 certificates are not the same thing. That's just dogfood for the corporate auditors.

At $work we are busy working on removing Tailscale. The bells and whistles of Tailscale sure are attractive candy, but we simply can't overlook the security risk of what has become a bloated product.
