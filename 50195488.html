Singing is about so much more than just hitting the right notes. Breath management is one of the most important skills you can develop to improve your singing. Proper vocal technique helps you avoid injuring your voice when tackling difficult songs. Learning to recognize and use techniques like belting, twang, head voice, chest voice, vocal fry, etc., can really bring your singing to life. Prosody and rhythm training help you develop good timing (one of the most important aspects of music), and there's so much more to it.

This kind of app is great for getting started and getting a taste of what singing involves, but it would be awesome to cover these other aspects as well, or at least mention their existence for aspiring singers.

You're not wrong. I do have a coding background which helps but I'm new to Rust. It started in Python with a web frontend so it somewhat evolved into this.

> why is it using webview/tauri?

Using these makes it easier to port to the other platforms later if I choose to.

About 365 of the 420 MB is the AI models which I decided to bundle so that the app wouldn't have to download additional things after the install.

My agent tells me that writing in native Swift would save (estimate) about 100–170 MB, out of about 1.4 GB when the models are active. And then I'd have to maintain the interface in two or three ways if I decided to make versions for other platforms.

If it does grow though and there are a lot of Mac users, this might be worth it though as you suggest. But I have a lot of project that go precisely nowhere so I always keep this in mind :)

HummingBirdGif|prev|next|

How are you detecting pitch?

dsmurrell|parent|next|

For the user's pitch OnKey uses CREPE-tiny (https://github.com/marl/crepe) but ported to Rust with the model running on ONNX Runtime. For the singer's pitch also CREPE but the full version.

The vocal separation for the song uses UVR: https://github.com/Anjok07/ultimatevocalremovergui

You can find more details here: https://singonkey.app/docs/how-it-works
