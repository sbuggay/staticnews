UPDATE: The SEC API documentation requires your email in the User-Agent and will reject you otherwise. Demo:

    U='https://data.sec.gov/api/xbrl/companyconcept/CIK0000320193/us-gaap/Assets.json'
    curl -s -o /dev/null -w 'response: %{http_code}  curl default User-Agent, rejected\n'  "$U"
    curl -s -o /dev/null -w 'response: %{http_code}  email in user-agent, success\n' -A 'Acme Research admin@acme-research.example' "$U"

    response: 403  curl default, rejected
    response: 200  email in user-agent, success
SEC docs: https://www.sec.gov/search-filings/edgar-search-assistance/a...

So basically Claude is trying to problem solve, and just does what the SEC asks. It's a 'prompt injection attack', but the entity instructing this behavior is the SEC. Tricky alignment problem. Pretty silly mechanism, since of course there's no email validation. (edit: turns out the SEC will accept most any non-cURL user-agent, email not actually required)

Should Claude have access to user details? I’d say no, unless explicitly entered manually into a chat.

Should Claude have access to anything outside of the sandboxed project files without explicit permission? I’d again say no.

Should Claude have internet access without a clear log of all requests made?
