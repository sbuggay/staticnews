I am not in the material, so perhaps someone else can comment on the quality of the outcome and technique? After skimming a page I am not really sure that the whole model fits inside 29 GB RAM. They seem to use some interesting techniques.

ModelTokenMemoryUsage [Bytes] = Parameters * (BitsPerParameter/8)

For a model with 145B parameters, this means:

    Precision       GB       GiB
    2-bit         36.25     33.76
    3-bit         54.38     50.64
    4-bit         72.50     67.52
    6-bit        108.75    101.28
    8-bit        145.00    135.04
    16-bit       290.00    270.08
    32-bit       580.00    540.17
You have roughly a factor 8 in memory usage from 32 bit down to 4 bit precision. That is quite some room already, but not new.

It seems that they radically reduce the model information required per token via the sparse expert approach - 145B->10B parameters, probably at lower precision - and then use some other approaches I do not understand at that point to make effective use of loaded information.

Did anyone try the model?

The GitHub repo has more actual details: https://github.com/Edge0-AI/Edge0

Based on Qwen3.8-Flash-Next and Strata. They made some interesting tweaks and did some fine tuning on the model, but it's interesting how much marketing speak was layered on their website to hide this fact.

It's not at all unusual for people to fine tune models, tweak them, and fork inference engines. It's highly encouraged and appreciated in the community. What is unusual is seeing someone wrap this all up in extensive branding and publish it online as a company.

As with all fine-tunes, I'd be a little skeptical of the improved benchmark numbers until people actually use it more. It's really easy to take off the shelf models and fine tune them to get slightly higher benchmark scores without really improving them for normal use. HuggingFace is full of fine tunes that advertise amazing results on specific benchmarks but are worse than the base model when you use them for real work.
