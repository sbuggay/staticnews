Author here. I reported twenty-seven .uk domains that used to belong to charities, universities and an NHS hospital and now serve unlicensed casino listings. Nominet told me to file them under "phishing" because none of its categories fit. That led to the question of who oversees the .uk registry, and the answer is nobody: no licence, no tender, no term, no regulator, unlike .fr, .eu and .us. The 2024 regulations that would let a minister intervene deliberately exclude content-based misuse, and since DSIT was abolished in July no department lists the powers in any minister's brief.

Two things from the registry's own RDAP that may interest people here. Twelve of the twenty-seven fail Nominet's own registrant validation, published in the record with a date, and are still live; one was renewed this month fifteen months after Nominet recorded that it could not identify the registrant. And the Gambling Commission refused under FOI to say how often it has used its new suspension power; Nominet then told the Guardian on Sunday the figure is 140.
