Nice article. There's another option for pixel art that's recently been coming into vogue as tool usage improves in LLMs which is procedural graphics.

I actually experimented with creating a set of skills around rendering early AGI style EGA graphical scenes, and it worked surprisingly well.

So rather than storing full bitmaps, you instead store sequences of simple drawing instructions designed to recreate some specific scene - like you would an SVG.

As a result you end up sidestepping a lot of the problems with larger generative models, which can have issues with grid alignment, palette choices, and high-frequency noise.

> Pixel art is a style, not a format

― person who has never attempted to program for a specific retro system.

Peep the graphics hardware limitations of the Nintendo Famicom/NES, for example: https://noelberry.ca/posts/nes/index.html

You can see these limitations guiding the designs of actual retro games if you know what to look for.

Example: In CAPCOM's Mega Man (1987), the famous “Yellow Devil” boss fight plays the way it does because only the moving chunks of the Devil's body are composed of Sprite tiles. When one of those chunks reaches the opposite side of the screen, that part of the Devil's body becomes drawn with Background tiles instead: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8RbPqqPrL_M

This is also why the Devil, Dragon, and Wily boss fights (anything larger than a regular Robot Master) take place entirely in a black void — the Background layer is exhausted drawing the bosses and there's nothing left over for actual play-area background.

vunderba|parent|prev|next|

> Whenever I try to run Image generation locally, be it with Flux or Qwen, I seem to be getting results that are about 1-2 years behind what ChatGPT can produce today.

At the risk of stating the obvious, you can't really compare local models to proprietary frontier models. For all we know, those models are heavily subsidized and have trillions more parameters than the smaller, modest ones you can run on a single GPU.

I've been running a prompt-adherence benchmark site [1] all the way back since the days of SDXL. If you look at the progress dropdown, I do think the gap is closing.

June of this year saw the release of some of the biggest open-weight bangers (Krea 2, Ideogram 4, and the kind of slept-on Boogu-Image 0.1), though the license terms can be kind of meh.

The best locally hosted model scores 8 out of 15, while the proprietary ones score 12 out of 15 on the text-to-image metric. If you want something that can reasonably decent for local gen while still being relatively small for modest GPUs, take a look at the recently released Moss-Image model [2]. But if you're expecting results at a level of something like GPT-Image-2.5 Sunburst, you're going to be disappointed.

[1] - https://genai-showdown.specr.net

[2] - https://huggingface.co/Comfy-Org/MOSS-Image
