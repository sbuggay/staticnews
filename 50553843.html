There is a real scenario, whose probability I cannot accurately assess, in which the first company to build "true" superintelligence will essentially capture the bulk of the entire US economy -- and that it will do so faster than any government can meaningfully intervene. This superintelligence is not a product sold in a chatbot, but rather the internal capability represented by their most advanced unreleased models, mostly without alignment, running on the world's largest GPU clusters, coordinating millions of copies. In this scenario, they don't capture the economy simply by selling tokens, but by owning the internal capability to create (or replicate) any technology, product, or digital business on-demand faster and more cheaply than any other organization on Earth. Selling chatbot tokens would just be the "bootloader" to a future phase in which Anthropic simply enters into and takes over most markets faster than anyone else can do anything about it.

If possible to do, this is a vastly superior strategy to selling tokens, since token consumers can swap out token vendors. But if you simply recreate their entire product and technology lineups from scratch, faster and cheaper, and capture all their customers with superior products and prices (at least at first), then you own the market rather than selling tokens into it amongst other token vendors.

None of this is guaranteed to happen. But I do not see a way to rule it out as a possibility.

No. While the scenario is speculative in the sense that it is not guaranteed to occur, it is not speculative in the sense of being science fiction. It is certainly not unfalsifiable. There are other scenarios in which this does not happen. For instance, the most obvious is that other organizations develop a similar capability at roughly the same point in time. This results in a "multipolar" economy that is mostly controlled by a small set of distinct owners of this kind of capability. The multipolarity prevents total capture and thereby prevents the rest of the scenario. Another obvious scenario is one in which the government acts soon enough to short-circuit this process before one organization gains a sufficiently decisive advantage that government intervention is no longer efficient enough to be effective. Yet another obvious scenario is one where a short-term financial crash deprives Anthropic of enough capital that this strategy is paused or slowed down enough to allow it to be countered. And yet another obvoius scenario is one where the scaling laws simply stop scaling or the amount of compute needed to scale them to this point cannot be physically obtained, thus allowing time for the strategy to be countered.

All of these alternatives would come with clear data points early on which would allow one to distinguish them from the original scenario I described. Right now, though, the data is ambiguous, and some data points lean slightly in favor of what I described, but not nearly enough to make any guarantees whatsoever.
