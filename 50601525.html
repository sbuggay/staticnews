Perplexity is not defined in the article. FYI:

Perplexity is the degree of surprise the model has in predicting the next token, over a sequence of tokens. At the worst case, perplexity is equal to the size of the vocabulary. At the best case, perplexity is 1.

So, given a sequence of tokens, "e = m c ^ 2", if the p(token | prev tokens) = 1 for every token, perplexity ends up being 1. If, however, it's a random predictor, the p(token | prev tokens) is 1/vocab for every token, and perplexity ends up being len(vocab).

The article plots log(perplexity) on the y-axis, which puts the best case at 0 (i.e. log 1).

gregdeon|root|parent|next|

The best possible perplexity is 1, and the best possible log-perplexity is log(1) = 0. The only way to achieve this is with a model that always predicts that the next token has probability 1 -- i.e., a perfect model.

A helpful comparison: suppose you have a model that always outputs a uniform distribution over K possible tokens (assigning probability 1/K to each one), and that the next token is indeed one of those K options. This model has perplexity K. So, in some sense a perplexity of K is comparable to narrowing down the answer to one of K possibilities. This includes the special case of K=1, which is the best possible model.

Why not?

If our objective is, for example, "compress the sum of all of humankind's knowledge", then we have a bit of a conundrum: that objective contains human knowledge of math, but also of religion, and of soap opera plots.

You, a human, could easily make an argument that one of those things is more important than the others, and thus should dominate. But an optimal compressor does not care. All of those things are in its objective. It weights them accordingly. It will compress both math and soap opera into its "universal information state" - and if it finds patterns that happen to help compress both, then all the better for it.

As such, if we take "compressor" as LLM and "objective" as its training datasets, a capable LLM would be able to both solve math problems and give a preacher-like sermon. But it would not let its knowledge of religion intrude into its performance in math, or the other way around, to an unwarranted level. The compressor lives by "non-overlapping magisteria."

Saturation means that the pretraining data has inherent entropy, even if we can't properly evaluate it. Absolutely the improvements in perplexity on the held-out OpenAI codebase dataset are due to more relevant samples in the training corpus and general improvements in data quality.

We assume that there is some point where the base models will reach the limits of inherent entropy for codebase quality. That there are fundamentally irreducible factors and unknowns that dictate resource-constrained and information-constrained performance.

Perplexity on any holdout dataset has limits to its usefulness as a measure and goes back to the eval and standardisation question.
