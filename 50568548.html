As someone else pointed out, training something like this requires much more hardware than running it.

It's similar with other forms of machine learning, too. A logistic regression can make calibrated decisions in complex situations with nearly no compute at all, but training it on lots of data to give it good weights is more expensive. And even if we could submit a training batch job on 1990s hardware and wait a few years for it to complete, there's a level of interactivity required at the start to explore one's way to the right parameters and tokenisation etc. that probably wouldn't have been economically feasible at the time.

In a wider sense, a general purpose computer is a general purpose computer. Hypothetically, we "could have" run transformers in the 1940s on vacuum tubes. It just would have (a) required much space, (b) produced much heat, and (c) required tube replacements all the time. We "could have" run transformers on human computers in the late 1800s or early 1900s – all the maths was there, I think? – but it would have been even slower!
