hunter2_|prev|next|

This is really cool. I have a suggestion, though: the samples within a button seem to have several modification types including a pitch shifter. For example, "DOWN4" under the "Multi-sampled sounds" heading sounds nearly a semitone lower than the other DOWN variants. I suggest eliminating all pitch shifting (or at least confining your use to a few cents), leaving only timbre variation. Go nuts creating variation through EQ, distortion, formant shifting, reflections/delay/reverb, etc. but keep the pitch locked. Why? Because the pitch of everything in this world is based on its resonant frequency, which is based on the size of whatever can vibrate, and in the case of a button it's resonating based on the size of the button. Many aspects of the sound would change based on things like how quickly the button is pushed or how much dampening the finger pushing it imparts, but the fundamental pitch is not one of them.

When playing a violin, if you move up the fingerboard, the pitch of the string increases. But if instead of playing the string you knock on the body as if knocking on someone's front door, the main pitch of that knocking never changes. The sympathetic vibrations of the strings will have frequencies that vary as the finger position varies, and the brightness and RT60 of the reverberation through the wood will vary from knock to knock, but the fundamental frequency generally excited by the knock only changes if the violin's volumetric size changes, and it never will.

Maybe a drum is a better example than a violin, since it's "unpitched" and yet still gets tuned to have some fundamental frequency. Only the noisy stuff varies from hit to hit, and the fundamental frequency does not (until you re-tune, or from temperature/humidity changing).

> Frustratingly, browsers can decide to block a website from playing any sound. iOS Safari seems particularly strict about this. So, if you’re not hearing a click sound when tapping the big red button, please check out this website on a desktop computer.

FYI, setting `navigator.audioSession.type` to `"playback"` will allow the sounds to play on iOS even when silent mode is on. (It defaults to `"ambient"`.)

On iOS versions below 16.4, this won't work as reliably; there are hacks you can do with `AudioContext`^[1] instead.

[1]: https://github.com/feross/unmute-ios-audio
