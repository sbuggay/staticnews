Ruling out other hypotheses is a perfectly legitimate way of doing science. But much of astrophysics is the other way around: we can’t interact with much of the universe but we can see lots of it, so we look for any evidence that a theory doesn’t hold.

Dark matter that only interacted gravitationally would have its own gravitational signature, which we can use to make predictions about what we should see, which would allow us to search for exceptions.

daynthelife|root|parent|next|

To be clear, hundreds of little red dots have beenconfirmed (e.g. via Balmer emission lines) not to be brown dwarfs and to genuinely be highly redshifted extragalactic bodies.

The confusion comes from the fact that initial surveys will scan for candidates by looking for objects with similar spectral energy distributions to LRDs. Since brown dwarfs show the same broad pattern in their spectral energy distributions, these datasets ended up being contaminated. This led to reports of "impossibly" redshifted objects (z>30) that turned out to just be brown dwarfs.

A proper emission line analysis gives 2-3 orders of magnitude more resolution than the coarse SED filter and (once performed) easily distinguishes the real LRDs from the brown dwarf imposters.
