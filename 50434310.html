It’s because it doesn’t have enough overtones, or a notable attack. You might have noticed that some trucks these days make a weird noise when they back up instead of a beep. They’re using white noise because it’s easier to locate; it has many overtones that allow your brain to pick the sound out of the background more easily, and thus more easily correlate the signals from both ears. You get a much more accurate location just from hearing that sound than you ever could with a pure tone.

akoboldfrying|root|parent|next|

Good point about the moving effort required. But if moving an object takes constant time, then it's O(n) overall -- since <incorrect argument deleted>.

EDIT: My earlier analysis was wrong (it assumed you never move the beeping item after the initial halving), but the conclusion was right for a different reason: Each time you move items, you move half as many as you did last time. You initially move n/2 items, and the geometric series n/2 + n/4 + n/8 + ... + 1 sums to n-1. So O(n) moves are done, and this dominates the log n term for the number of steps.
