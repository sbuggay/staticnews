Citrus ancestry is stupidly complex, and the generally commercially available varieties sadly few.

You may have heard of yuzu, which was a foodie fad a couple of years ago (and deservedly so, it smells & tastes great). In Japan, it's only one of numerous citruses, including dekopon (navel mandarins, occasional sold in Australia as "sumo"), tachibana, kabosu, sudachi, etc.

Hop a few islands down to Okinawa, and the fruits change yet again! Here you have shikwasa (hirami lemon), tachibana, tanibuta, etc.

An incomplete list on Wikipedia: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japanese_citrus

thaumasiotes|root|parent|next|

> Nobody does that work to make money on a poster.

You reminded me of something. I was looking at a book of artwork with my brother and he commented that some of the art was bad because there was too much drawn in the background.

I think this is a case of "misplaced advice" - the belief that background artwork lowers the overall quality of a piece is false, but it persists because it is helpful to artists.

If you're trying to sell your artwork, people will pay a lot more for foregrounds than they will for backgrounds, but backgrounds aren't any easier than foregrounds - generally they're harder - so any time you spend drawing stuff in the background effectively lowers your hourly earnings. (Unless the level of background detail in your art is so low that it's making it all unsaleable.)

But if you ask people about what artwork they like, background detail is always helpful.
