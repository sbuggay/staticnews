Backup is a trust business. The entire product promise is "your data is safe with us for years", and customers pay monthly for that promise whether or not they ever restore anything. Monetising the same customer data for advertising reads, to many customers, as the opposite of that promise, whatever the legal fine print says.

The opt-out pattern makes it worse. When a company with a retention-based model introduces data sharing and hides the off switch, it converts a quiet pricing or growth problem into a visible trust problem. Churn in backup products is normally low and slow; moves like this give a loyal, low-maintenance customer base an active reason to shop around, and backup is exactly the kind of product where switching, once started, tends to complete.

If the ad revenue per user is small relative to lifetime value, the maths on this looks bad even before reputational damage.
