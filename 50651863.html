Abstract: We propose a background independent quantization of vacuum general relativity on four dimensional Lorentzian spacetimes. Starting from first order gravity, we solve the gravitational constraints on intersecting null cones of a bulk subregion and obtain the reduced phase space at finite GN. We promote its canonical Poisson brackets to commutation relations and use deformations of the reduced action to define an algebra of observables. Equipped with this, we use Gaussian wavefunctions of canonical pairs to construct a nonperturbative Hilbert space representation, for which this algebra generates a type I factor. By composing amplitudes between states prepared on null cones, we then obtain a Lorentzian path integral in which each sum over intermediate states converges absolutely. As a check, we compute the amplitude for graviton pair creation and annihilation on a null cone nonperturbatively in GN and find a finite closed form expression, even though expanding first in GN yields a logarithmic divergence. We next use diffeomorphism invariance of the path integral to show that a macroscopic causal diamond with a given maximum size has a finite dimensional Hilbert space. From the Lorentzian trace we obtain the Bekenstein Hawking entropy for marginally trapped surfaces and calculate its logarithmic correction for large Schwarzschild black holes, recovering at one loop the coefficient Sen obtained through Euclidean methods. We end by deriving the semiclassical Einstein equations to one loop as the low energy limit of this theory.

I'm just going off the abstract as a lay person with interest in these things, but the idea seems neat and clean at first blush.

Spacetime is 3+1 dimensions, and null cones are the slices that a pulse of light traces out over it's lifetime. Really, light is just a stand-in for "thing that travels at the speed of causality", so null cones define regions of spacetime that are causally connected. Anything outside that region would have to send matter or signals faster than light otherwise.

Chandrasekaran claims that looking at how several of these regions intersect lets them derive algebraic constraints on how gravity in general relativity behaves. They say that this yields a useful path integral, which is just an equivalent alternative to using wave functions in quantum mechanics.

The compelling part is using that to show several exact calculations of things that are traditionally hard, with the prize being a claimed derivation of general relativity as a special case, in a similar way to how Newtonian gravity is a special case of general relativity.

adrian_b|root|parent|next|

Those are not predictions, they were constraints used in the derivation of this theory.

Known facts can be considered as predictions of a new theory only when the theory is formulated in a way that is completely independent of those facts and then the known facts appear as consequences of the new theory, even if there is no logical reason for this to happen, which demonstrates that those facts were really superfluous, being derivable from a smaller number of premises.

For example, a new theory of gravitation could show that the existence of dark matter is a superfluous hypothesis, if it could explain the star movements without needing dark matter.

That would indeed predict a measurable fact that has not been predicted by the existing gravity theory.

Or a new theory of gravity could compute the Newtonian constant of gravity, instead of having to measure it experimentally, like in the present theory. This would reduce the number of fundamental constants that must be determined empirically.

So any prediction of a new theory must reduce the number of arbitrary facts that exist in the present theory. As long as that number stays the same, the new theory is at best a mathematically-equivalent formulation of the old theory. In the worst case, the new theory has additional arbitrary parameters that cannot be determined experimentally, so it is more complex than the old theory without providing any benefit.

adrian_b|root|parent|next|

For now there exists no evidence whatsoever that space-time is quantized, i.e. discrete instead of continuous.

This means for example that there is no evidence whatsoever that anyone of the primitive quantities time, distance, angle and action is discrete and not continuous.

(Nota bene: there are a lot of physics textbooks that incorrectly mention the existence of a so-called "quantum of action". That is a huge mistake which is the cause of many confusions and erroneous results. Action is a quantity that for any physical system increases monotonically with time. Therefore it cannot be constant much less quantized. Angular momentum, which is the ratio between action and plane angle, can be quantized in many physical systems, where its quantum can be either Planck's constant or its half. There is nothing mysterious about the quantization of the angular momentum, it expresses a resonance condition between 2 rotation movements. Planck's constant, being energy per frequency, where energy is action per time and frequency is plane angle per time, is an angular momentum, not an action. Action is a true scalar, invariant at coordinate changes. For a particle, action is the integral of the Lagrangian over its world line in spacetime.)

All the traditional quantum physics and quantum field theory are based on the continuity of space-time, which is necessary for having any differential equations.

There have been some tentative theories where space-time is treated as a discrete lattice, but they have not succeeded in providing any useful results.

(The arbitrary discretizations of the space-time that are used to obtain approximate solutions of integro-differential equations are very different from having a discrete space-time as the fundamental theory, because in the latter case the discretization would have to be unique and with measurable parameters).
