adrian_b|root|parent|next|

The problem is that fluoridation of the drinking water is not supported by any science.

The scientific facts are:

1. The beneficial effect of fluoride occurs only when fluoride is applied externally, in contact with the tooth enamel.

2. Fluoride that is ingested has only harmful known effects inside the body.

3. Once fluoride is ingested, the only way for it to have an effect on tooth enamel is to exit again the body in saliva.

4. Of the amount of ingested fluoride, only an extremely small fraction will reach the secreted saliva.

5. Any effect of fluoride during the few seconds while drinking or in the minute concentration that can exist in saliva must be much smaller than the effect of concentrated fluoride in toothpaste or mouth washes, which is also in contact with the teeth for a longer time.

6. The exact amount of fluoride that would be ingested in the drinking water is unpredictable, because the amount of water will depend on many factors, like body size, what kind of food is eaten, ambient temperature, amount of physical activity. Because also the fraction that would be excreted in saliva is unknown, it is completely impossible to optimize the amount of fluoride in drinking water for a desired effect on the teeth.

7. Even if it would possible to determine an adequate amount of fluoride in drinking water, that would remain an extremely inefficient method, because almost all fluoride is wasted because it does not reach tooth enamel, except for a few seconds during drinking and for the very small fraction secreted in saliva.

The extreme inefficiency of drinking water fluoridation, coupled with the associated health risks, allow one to conclude that from a scientific point of view it is beyond any reasonable doubt that the right way to use fluoride is in toothpaste or mouth washes, not in drinking water.
