logicallee|prev|next|

There are a few more requirements:

- You may no longer send mail pretending to be a real person or company that you're not.

In the past, it was possible to set yourself as any sender. This is no longer allowed.

- You may no longer send out canvassing emails that purport to express a business interest, investor interest, collaboration, or job offer if you are not prepared to immediately follow through.

That means no more job spam and scams where you are in no way in any position or have any interest in hiring someone.

Likewise, you may no longer send fake investor spam that you're interested in investing in someone, whereas in reality you have zero ability or interest in doing so.

Interfering with other people's business relationships has always been a serious offense. It is now a criminal liability. If someone is doing business with someone else, reaching out, or starting to, you may not under threat of criminal liability in any way interfere with that relationship - not even through attempting to influence or manipulate the recipient.

Regarding blocking or screening of certain mail that could be legitimate but may be lower value than some other mail that is more important: currently there is no guidance that every single sent or received mail must go through 100% of the time. We value that there is a certain bar or threshold, because when there isn't then it's a hundred spam calls a day, a hundred spam emails, evverything is spam.

However, the advice is, therefore, when something is allowed through, then you should be prepared to allow it to progress naturally. It doesn't make sense to carefully curate relationships and what gets through, only to stop them if the recipient attentively reads the curated feed and engages with the sender on the terms they've set.

I really hate when I take the time to respond to someone who reached out to me, and then my response goes to a black hole.

Thank you for improving the Internet and cutting down on spam and scams.
