devilbunny|root|parent|next|

There's not a "test" in the sense of a blood draw for pancreatic cancer. Diagnosis is made by getting a piece of tissue (not easy) and having a pathologist review it.

At a bare minimum, you will need to have a mass large enough to be seen on CT scan, and then an endoscopic biopsy (among GI scopes, the endoscopic retrograde cholangiopancreatography or ERCP is one of the most difficult). And even that may not get enough tissue for a diagnosis.

So, yeah, failure to diagnose it is common - largely because it's almost impossible to diagnose early. I'm a doctor. We feel sick when these things happen. It's horrible for our patients.

So many impacted by cancer in this thread, I've also been in the position where you feel powerless when someone close to you is diagnosed. I try to take opportunities like this to remind people that there are some types of cancer (and other conditions) that you can help other people with through a bone marrow transplant.

Cancers like leukemia, melanoma, lymphoma and conditions like aplastic anemia, immune, autoimmune disorders can be treated by infusing the patient with compatible bone marrow stem cells from a donor. Finding a compatible donor can be difficult, on average in the general population its on the order of 1:10,000 to find a match. For that reason there's a registry that people can submit a cheek swab to. In my case I joined the registry about 15 years ago when someone in my extended friend circle had leukemia. A swab drive was organized to find her a donor, which eventually happened and she wound up beating it. I didn't match her but a few years ago I matched another patient and wound up donating.

In the US the registry is National Marrow Donor Program. Signup on their website and they will send you a free swab kit.

https://www.nmdp.org/
