QGQBGdeZREunxLe|root|parent|next|

The ball is forced downwards when the trigger is pulled because liquid initially flows towards the dip tube, moving the ball onto the valve seat. Once seated, the pressure of the liquid above the ball keeps it firmly in place.

The illustration is not great because it looks like the ball forms a watertight seal in its upper position, rather than simply hitting a mechanical stop. This distinction is better illustrated in the original patent diagram [0].

Once the trigger is released, reduced pressure in the chamber causes liquid to flow up the dip tube, effectively reloading the sprayer.

[0] - https://patents.google.com/patent/US4527741

I'm neither a mechanical engineer nor a physicist, so feel free to correct my understanding.

If you do it the long way - disassembling the object, modeling one in a cad program, making it move, and building a web page that explains how it works - the you have learnt a few things:

* you have, most obviously, learnt something about how this particular machine you are documenting works

* you have practiced your CAD; maybe every part was trivial to model, maybe some were an interesting challenge that made you finally see what a tool you precious saw no use for is for, maybe you found a way to do something faster, you’re a little better at CAD in one way or another

* you have practiced writing text that succinctly and precisely describes the action of the object, which will make you slightly better at communicating clearly in any situation

You have gained experience in multiple disciplines. The webpage is the excuse to have the experience of understanding these surprisingly complex little machines, and fooling around with the tools to document this understanding.

Perhaps in the future you will use this knowledge to design some small, complex machines of your own, starting by lying back and modeling their parts and actions entirely in your mind, then taking them to the CAD software, printing them, experimenting with them in reality, seeing what doesn’t work as expected, thinking about how it could work better, and making a new version.

Perhaps all of this could be done by an AI. But you would have not gained any experience in any of these skills. You would not have had the experience of watching yourself get better at a thing, you would not have whiled away hours with a hobby. You just would have the end result.

Tl;dr: The journey can matter much more than the destination.
