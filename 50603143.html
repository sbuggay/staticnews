> If you do not write for other people, stop reading this now.

This is the opening line. But in my view this is part of what defines a blog. You write for yourself and share hoping others will enjoy it, as well.

An overwhelming part of the time I feel the post title and opening paragraph tells me whether a piece is for me, without the author having to explain it explicitly.

I've been blogging for a few years myself: one post every few months. They are long pieces which I pour a ridiculous amount of effort into. And I do it for myself, even though the ostensible goal is to explain something I found interesting in my work to anyone who might be interested. And I put a lot of effort into making it accessible. The only time I've explicitly mentioned who it is for happens to be my latest and by far most popular article. However, I see that as an accident: a lot of people asked for a more nitty gritty focus. I obliged, adding a disclaimer that it was written for a different target audience than the rest. Turns out it was a better fit for my writing.

> a blogger should just write however he feels like

On principle I agree, with one caveat: you still should communicate to your audience, even if it is that of one. If you just write to organise ypur thoughts never intending for anyone, yourself included, to read them afterwards, fine, but perhaps in that case a physical journal would be a better option anyway? I write a blog to in which I scribe intentionally boring things, simply because I know I will forget at least some of them in due time. I doubt another basic tutorial on SQL is any value to anyone. It is to myself, however, since it is written by me in my words. instead of looking up bunch of commands I use so rarely I will forget them anyway, I might as well write myself a tutorial on how to use them in the future.

However, if I just list them haphazardly like in a private notebook, it will be filled with typos, errors and otherwise difficult to read. If I write it pretending I am writing on New York Times, my future self will thank me, since he will actually easily understand what the hell I was trying to say.

So even if you write just to yourself, you should write to an audience who does not have the knowledge and experiences you have. Even if the audience is yourself, but months or years removed, he will likely have forgotten all of that and value you have written them down plainly and clearly.

I think bloggers should mostly write how they feel like. Yes, there’s an audience, or a potential audience, but you calculate how much you want to optimize for the audience and how much to optimize for other qualities.

Meh. I think this is very good advice when writing in corporate settings. Get to the point, be concise, connect with what the reader actually cares about because they don't have the time or the inclination to geek out about your thing.

For blogging, not so much. Find your niche, sure, but that niche can be pretty eclectic. The internet is so vast that you might very well be able to connect with 100 or 1,000 or 10,000 people who like unusual topics or writing styles. It's actually harder if you're just trying to do something very mainstream because there's already a million blogs written in a corporate style.

The main sin of blogging is something else: writing blog posts like they're chapters of a book when many readers are first-time visitors or no longer remember your previous article. If people need to re-read the previous post to even understand what the current one is about, and then that previous post in turn references an even earlier one, you're gonna lose a lot of readers.

On the flip side, in 2026, I'm not going to tell any budding blogger to just write for themselves, because that's just doing free labor for OpenAI. So yeah, make some effort to understand who your audience is or could be.
