I'm surprised how well this handled. I usually have large complaints on the flight dynamics in projects like these, and for this (in expert mode) they were not that large. Of course, it's no X-Plane, but I'm trying to keep in mind what sort of project it is. In a good blend of arcade and simulation, it should be a toy that is easy to get into, but someone who has simulator experience should also feel at home and not run into counter-intuitive dynamics.

I suppose I am a little surprised the biplane was able to loop on its own power from level flight.

Interaction-wise, I would have liked

- to be able to invert the Y axis on the mouse control! Nuts that this alternative is not included.

- a vertical speed gauge all the time, not just when the simulator thinks I'm about to land.

- go-arounds to be handled more gracefully, rather than as a very long landing.

- a trim control on the mouse wheel to set the airspeed. (And then the mouse control could keep returning to centre, at least in expert mode.)

- a toggle for opting out of metric units, and instead using more customary knots for speed, feet for altitude, feet per minute for vertical speed etc.

- pre-tuned navigation radios (or even just VFR charts) as an optional replacement for arrows in the sky.

With these changes (and possibly more I think of later) this could be a really fun way to get children into flight simulation, since it's so action packed.

There are tons of seed generated examples from Claude (and others)

Here's mine:

https://greggman.github.io/gen-city-auto/ (430k, 4 cites, tons of vehicles, trains, signs, water, bridges, rain, road details) - video for highlights: https://x.com/greggman/status/2117605120745881692

https://greggman.github.io/cyber-web-city/ (200k, 10 cities themes) - video for highlights: https://x.com/greggman/status/2109451367941378213

https://greggman.github.io/webgpu-driving/ (170k - 10 environments, several vehicles, tunnels) - video for highlights: https://x.com/greggman/status/2104533425478930568

https://greggman.github.io/webgpu-aquarium/ (102k - several fish, different ocean styles)

note: iOS in particular is really slow to start up these.

Maybe it's all AI slop. None of them are fun games, they're just tech demos.

The interesting things are they are all small. All assets are built at startup. They aren't using any libraries. That suggests there is lots of low-hanging fruit for quick to start web-games.

Telling the LLM to build from scratch and follow AAA practices frees the LLM to use whatever is appropriate, rather than whatever some library dictates. The LLM was also good at optimizing, at least to an extent. It would run experiments, turning features on/off to find where the speed was going. It would also do A/B screenshots to see how much changing techniques effected the output.

Each demo from prompt to first output took 2-3 hours and then a few prompts in my free time to clean up the most outstanding issues.

One thing that helped a bunch was telling the LLM to model cars using NURBS instead of polygons. Another was telling it to have a separate modeling agent, and a separate judging agent.

None of the demos are perfect. They got to a point where I was happy that they showed want I wanted to see. I wasn't planning to take them to perfection. The gen-city-auto demo has the most issues. While it made a giant city, there are lots of places where a road makes no sense. I could prompt through that but all I really wanted to see was a GTA like city in a small amount of code and for that it proved the concept is valid. Given ways for a designer to declare what they wanted where I could see the concept generate much more "designed" cities.
