Related: I've been finding a lot of success (defined as more autonomy with less quality dropoff) with team-based workflows.

A lot of evidence for this.

- Adaptive Auto-Harness: Sustained Self-Improvement for Agentic System Deployment on Open-Ended Task Streams (https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.01770) talks about multi-agent, role-based adaptive harnesses collaborating on non-homogeneous workstreams

- team-solve (https://github.com/guregodevo/memdoor/tree/main/examples/wor...) team of small models beats Fable on LiveCodeBench

- Agensh paper (https://arxiv.org/html/2609.26781v1) also talks about "role-playing agents to study autonomous cooperation"

All show that role-based, multi-agent setups improve performance.

Here is a short list of example results reported on the post; again we will have dedicated Antigravity posts for some of these.

* Math Open Problems & Formal Verification: Reporting 10+ open math and CS theory problems solved including 50M-line Lean 4 proof of Courtade–Kumar conjecture plus progress on several open problems in quantum query complexity, Tarjan's Deque Conjecture.

  Also autonomous Lean formalizations of the Weil (1.8M lines), Birkhoff, and 3D Bochner–Riesz conjectures via Teamwork-LEAP, plus updates on Deep Review for STOC’27.
* Chemistry & Discovery: 95% top-1 exact match on NMR molecular structure elucidation (nearly 2× single-agent). Also empowering Nuclear Fusion and Quantum algorithm results as well autonomously generating a CausalAnalyst Benchmark. In addition, we show how /teamwork can start from Alphazero paper, implement a competitive go game, and autonomously improving it by playing with KataGo to get to the medium Elo level.

* Several Systems, TPU Kernel, & Infra: * Clustering 1-Trillion-edge graphs on FarArray and RDMA (up to 180× faster), * 800K-line Zircon & 170K-line libgav1 safe Rust rewrites (20–30% faster than C++/ASM), * 4.09× JAXBench TPU kernel speedups with DuoDiscover, * Google internal examples: surpassing 1 PiB fleet memory savings, and 24% fewer Borg TPU preemptions for Borg Scheduling.
