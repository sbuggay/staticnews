I mention this a lot: the two basic post-processing steps you have to do, at a minimum, to give genai images even a semblance of pixel art are palette reduction and square grid alignment.

Unfortunately, the definition of "pixel art" like "vibe coding" has been diluted to the point of almost being meaningless.

If you have a project like this with tens of thousands of tiles, you have to run them all through square grid alignment. That process tries to pick an optimal square grid and then reduce the image to a discrete set of pixels, with each square resolved to a single color.

It's similar to what you do when you shrink an image using nearest-neighbor scaling. As a result, you mitigate a lot of the irritating high-frequency noise and fringing that occur in these higher-resolution pixel art images from generative AIs.

Applying it to a project with tens of thousands of images still requires a lot of manual intervention. This is what makes separates good pixel art from bad pixel art: even at a low resolution of 16x16 or 32x32, it's still recognizable.

Try taking any of the 512x512 tiles from this particular isometric city project (or any of the other dozen variants) and feed'em into Spritefusion and you'll quickly see what I mean.

Pixel art is generally made out of well-defined pixels that are all on the same grid in the same scale. When you zoom in, the pixels look sharp. Pixel art usually rarely has anti-aliased lines, whereas every line in Isometric SF is anti-aliased.

Comparison of how lines look with vs without anti-aliasing:

- https://media.geeksforgeeks.org/wp-content/uploads/202307201...

Examples of "real" pixel art:

- https://www.slynyrd.com/blog/2022/11/28/pixelblog-41-isometr...

- https://www.behance.net/gallery/18524751/Pixel-Art-City

- https://platform.theverge.com/wp-content/uploads/sites/2/cho... (this may look blurry if you zoom in or have a monitor display scale other than 1x, but when viewed properly the pixels are sharp)

- https://retrodiffusion.ai/explore/5818 (AI generated, but the output was constrained to make it have pixels, instead of having downscaled smooth lines that look anti-aliased)

Examples of "fake" pixel art:

- https://media.easy-peasy.ai/61515f6c-78d2-4786-95ec-ccada6e5... (the pixels vary in size and shape, and are blurry)

- https://media.easy-peasy.ai/c558c867-167c-4f11-afed-c9f85f2a... (same issue)

- https://easy-peasy.ai/ai-image-generator/images/pixel-art-of... (you can see at the very bottom of the apple, the pixels are half the height they are supposed to be)
