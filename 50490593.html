Hi all. Thanks for the interest. I'm going to post an impromptu blog post here just explaining what this is. The rather unfortunate ground stop today made this unexpectedly useful with the pilots here on Orcas just to get a sense of the chatter in our airspace re the situation.

This is an ETTUS B210 attached to a dual 4090 box. It's a polyphase channelizer that takes the entire airband, channelizes it using cuda code on GPU, demodulates it, detects transmissions, throws away data tx and then feeds the tx through a voice model I created for transcription. A separate LLM reads the transcriptions to create a situational awareness picture of the airspace.

I'm interested in open sourcing this and making it available for folks who can run an ETTUS B210 or high quality SDR with enoguh bandwidth and a local GPU machine, so that we can cover more airpsace.

This was born out of an incident we had not too long ago where a pilot around KORS (based at Friday Harbor) lost the engine in his 172, and put it in the water in front of a ferry and was picked up by the ferry. We're a pretty close bunch on Orcas Island and we only heard about it days later via social media. That felt completely unacceptable, and I set out to create a radio that could monitor everything in real-time, all the time, and alert on emergencies. That has extended to transcribing and creating situational awareness for a chunk of airspace.

I'm an IFR pilot. I fly a T206H regularly in IMC around here and I'm a ground instructor. You can email me at mark@defiant.com or find me on x at mmaunder if you'd like to connect. I'm particularly interested in hearing from folks who can run a station on their own hardware and are under bravo or a charlie or even just somewhere interesting re airspace.

amluto|root|parent|next|

I would expect that the hardware involved is massive overkill.

The Ettus SDRs are very nice and are marked up enormously over the BOM cost. You’re paying for an integrated, supported system, with good engineering, not for the parts. These days, for this particular use, you could probably build your own direct (no downconversion) receiver with a decent filter and, say, a 400MSPS DAC. (This was not a thing at any reasonable price point 15 years ago when I last played with this stuff.) Or you can buy or build a little mixer and choose an appropriate LO frequency and use a much cheaper DAC. Or you can buy a cheaper SDR.

~15 years ago I amused myself for about an hour using a USRP (older Ettus gear) and MATLAB to do more or less this but for regular FM radio, one channel at a time, on a decent laptop of the era. The processing power needed to channelize and demodulate a few channels from I/Q data is very, very low. Even coming up with some kind of optimal demodulation scheme wouldn’t use many cycles. (Although I suspect that there is more space to try to jointly optimize demodulation and transcription with FM than with AM.)

It would be a fun project to try to kludge up an ESP32 of some sort to do this. The tricky bit would be that the WiFi/BT hardware doesn’t go to DC.
