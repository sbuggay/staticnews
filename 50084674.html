tptacek|root|parent|next|

Secure messaging is its own (increasingly well-defined) cryptographic problem. Group secure messaging is a distinct problem with its own cryptography. Backups are their own problem with their own constraints. Package signatures are their own problem.

There isn't one cryptosystem that does all these things. For messaging you want a carefully-defined notion of forward secrecy and post-compromise recovery, along with a careful decision about repudiation/non-repudation (1990s cryptography doctrine would suggest you just always want nonrepudiabilty, which turns out to be disastrous for privacy). For backup, you want deduplication and content addressing. For packages, you want transparency logs. You almost never want long-lived keys, but you often want identify binding.

PGP solves basically none of these problems, and offers instead a 1998 API (I'm not kidding, I thought about what year to pin it at) to basic cryptography primitives.

At least the versions I remember had a really bad UX for marking keys as trusted.

The correct operation for (even locally) marking "this key belongs to person X" is signing the key. Marking it as "trusted" indicates "this key is a CA that can create statements who other keys belong to", but it's easier to find/"understand".

So if you haven't first studied the details of the security model and terminology, you just start increasing the trust level on the key of your communication partner until the warnings go away, not realizing that by doing this, you've made that key a CA and the reason the warnings disappeared is because you now trust the self-signature (in addition to all other signatures the key issued).

Joel_Mckay|root|parent|prev|next|

In general, a session/thread ephemeral key pair is exchanged with the participants, and only each users private origin signing validates each sender sections. Since the signatures are passively checked, it does not need redone after each append operation.

Thus, participants are pooled under 1 time constrained key exchange, participants sections origins are signed/time-stamped, and the ciphertext expires on a schedule.

Why? so at some future time side-channel key exfiltration can't be used to data mine the entire history of past tapped conversations/ciphertext. Even if a private signing key is stolen, it is difficult to rebuild the lost ephemeral key session.
