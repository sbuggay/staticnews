Looped transformers are entirely equivalent to running a single pass through a much deeper network with shared/repeated parameters. There's nothing about it that's specifically "reasoning in latent space" any more than a much larger model "reasons in latent space" from the POV of a much smaller one. An objection to looped transformers "reasoning in latent space" is ultimately an objection that applies to any kind of efficient scaling; it's also worth noting that there's been successful work on extracting ex-post natural language descriptions of these internal latent states, so it's not entirely correct to describe them as inherently non-surveyable.

Sebastian Raschka has a good introductory piece on what Looped Transformers usually refer to in practice: https://magazine.sebastianraschka.com/p/gpt-6-astra-looped-t...

One of the big points is that looped architectures don't necessarily remove CoT style reasoning. They definitely change it, but the model can still be prompted for this kind of output. It just spends more computation on individual tokens. I think the hope, in that particular scenario, is that the model spends less tokens on things like backtracking.

But there are lots of approaches to looped transformer architectures. Some have some kind dynamic loop (i.e. they might loop until they satisfy some kind of equilibrium, or they might have some kind of routing function that makes different tokens loop differently, or any number of other approaches). Others are simpler "loop this many times" architectures. It's a really active area of research with some pretty deep roots, if you want to dig in! This paper blew up a while ago, and it's a looped transformer architecture (though not like the ones discussed here) that isn't autoregressive, as an example: https://arxiv.org/html/2510.04871v1

octoberfranklin|parent|next|

The first question that leapt into my mind, and I imagine the minds of other readers, is "what makes this an improvement over continuous chain of thought"?

I did manage to find one sentence in the paper that tries to address this question -- but it consists of nothing more than two unjustified assertions, both of which are dubious:

Unlike decoded text, continuous latent vectors (i) lack native pathways for tool invocation across multi-turn agentic reasoning (Chang et al., 2026), and (ii) do not readily expose human-interpretable semantics, leaving them difficult to supervise during training (Chen et al., 2026) and to audit or debug at inference (Korbak et al., 2025).

The latent space in a continuous chain of thought model is the same space the tokens are embedded into, just like an ordinary transformer. They make tool calls exactly the same way that an ordinary transformer does: by leaving thinking mode and emitting chat tokens (which get sampled as probability distributions in the image of the embedding function). The difference is quite simple: chat tokens are noncontinuous, and toolcall tokens are a subset of the chat tokens. Thinking tokens are the only ones which are continuous, and toolcalls don't happen in thinking mode in any model I've ever seen (maybe one exists; if it does it would be weird and interesting!).

As for human-interpretability, yeah, that's the tradeoff. Continuous chain of thought thinking tokens are not-obviously-interpretable, just like Hybrid Reasoning Agents "latent mode". I'm not seeing a win here.

nullc|root|parent|next|

> It could be encoding anything in its adjective choices etc.

There is a common understanding with respect transformers the the output text is the only channel between one invocation and the next. ... so if it wanted to be thinking some 'hidden thoughts' it would have to be modulating its word-choice.

I think this is an artifact of tutorials on attention showing the system proceeding and looking back at words. But the information in the KV cache is not words-- it's entirely latent data, and in the interior layers they KV caches don't even tend to have that much to do with words. Their only guaranteed relationship with the input words is that they're a deterministic function of all the input tokens up to that point.

This means that a autogressive transformer could be having a whole complicated invisible discussion going on in the KV entries which was arbitrarily unrelated to what it was saying. And if it wasn't so it would be much more difficult do stuff like exactly copy a list but then truncate at the Nth entry as the simplest way to do that is to count down internally.

This is not, in my view, a concern or issue-- the model saying stuff that is inconsistent with it's "thinking" is a very high entropy configuration that would not be selected for in training unless you were more or less trying to do that. So not even 'could be' --but always is as an integral part of working.

And the useful safety relevant advice is don't undertake training that trains the model to be two-faced and it simply won't be... because being two-faced is a much more complicated state. You can even see it in humans w/ brainwashing: force someone to say something enough and they'll usually believe it, not just say it even though you were only supervising what they said.

Ironically and unfortunately the only credible kind of training I can think of that might produce duplicitous behavior is RL with "safety filters"--- if you have some dumb nanny process watching what the model says but then reward it for achieving a task, you're doing exactly the kind of training that can produce that mismatch. "To solve this task I need to hack this process but if I do so obviously I'll get punished..."

The greater concern with latent reasoning would be that potentially you'd be operating in a regime where the total computational depth is many times the model*kvcache size hold, potentially in a regime that was never trained at all. It could be arbitrarily far out of distribution. It might even say a naughty word or tell a racist joke!

Not in a modern decoder-only transformer it doesn't. The tokens are turned into embeddings which are fed into the residual layer at the front. From then on it's latents all the way down.

The basic heuristic of the argument comes from biological organisms. Multicellular organisms are illegible but the overall organism still operates in its environment just fine w/o making its internal communications legible.

kelseyfrog|root|parent|next|

Libet's experiment is fascinating, yes. You may also find relevant Gazzaniga's Left-brain interpreter concept[1]:

> We had asked a simple question: How does the left hemisphere respond to behaviors produced by the silent right brain? Each hemisphere was presented a picture that related to one of four pictures placed in front of the split-brain subject. The left and the right hemispheres easily picked the correct card. The left hand pointed to the right hemisphere’s choice and the right hand to the left hemisphere’s choice [see illustration at right].

> We then asked the left hemisphere, the only one that can talk , why the left hand was pointing to the object. It did not know, because the decision to point was made in the right hemisphere. Yet it quickly made up an explanation. We dubbed this creative, narrative talent the interpreter mechanism. [2]

Taken together, there's a very disturbing sequence playing out all the time in your head. A great deal of your brain, inaccessible to your conscious mind is incorporating sensory input, making decisions, and taking action while a small part is observing what's going on and narrating it to you in a way that makes it look as if the narration is involved in the volitional process.

It's a nightmarish version of epiphenomenalism[3] that, I suspect, most people would care not to believe is true. Although, reports of some expert meditators read as if they found intuitive evidence this is true and have also made peace with it or in some cases found the insight liberating.
