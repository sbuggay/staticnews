Cool. I like the swiping. Very satisfying.

I made my own flashcard thing a few months ago, when I realized my goal was the opposite of Anki's goal.

Anki — and all flashcard software these days — makes learning as efficient as possible, which they define as minimizing learning time. It does this by showing you things as rarely as possible.

Unfortunately that also maximizes pain, effort and forgetting, and minimizes fluency. I wanted the opposite of that, because I am lazy and don't like effort, pain and failure. (I also like fluency!)

The goal of minimizing time makes sense for a law student or someone in med school, but for me that's not a problem I actually have, and it creates all these other problems.

(Flashcard software is also known to have pretty bad user retention, which if I had to guess is due to the fact that it is effectively designed to be painful.)

So I just made a thing oriented around the exact opposite goal: showing me cards as often as possible! (And turns out I was half a century late with this idea :) https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Precision_teaching

Then I realized I could just modify Anki instead of maintaining an entire software, so I did that instead.

Then I realized I am just too lazy to do flashcards, and creating new software did not solve that problem. To make the reps easy and to maximize fluency. (The quest continues... maybe I should try again when I get some Ritalin!)

p.s. I did a deep dive on this stuff and discovered that what we call "overlearning" is actually just what used to be called learning not so long ago, and that what we call "mastery learning" is actually temporary basic competence.
