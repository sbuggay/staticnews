Crazy to not see a word about benchmarking/evaluation of the kernels.

IMO at this time when all performant GPU kernels are generated/hill-climbed, its the largest source of error we see.

Should be kernel run with a hot or cold cache in practice? Should your kernel be bitwise equivalent to a ref? What error margins should your kernel have to ref? How many times are you running the kernel to avoid thermal noise? Are you running on separate GPUs to avoid overfitting the current hardware? Can you cudagraph-compiling your kernel to remove launch overhead? Are you A/B B/A interleaving your kernel/reference runs to avoid running thermal bias?

augment_me|root|parent|next|

It depends on if you do training or inference and also at what scale.

For your task with the 3090, and I assume inference, I would like you suggest do end-to-end on target task.

Sometimes for example, a normalization layer can cancel out/fix an error from a previous layer, then it makes no sense to spend flops to save the numerics.

This is kind of a wild west in inference serving, you sometimes change exp to tanh, sometimes go full 4bit, sometimes remove full layers because you still perform fine on the end-task.

Numerics and correctness are more important for training kernels and backwards passes
