wyager|prev|next|

The point of shooting in a log format isn't to make the footage look a certain way; it's to reduce the degree to which quantization/clipping throws away information you want to have in post.

If you film with a standardized transfer function like HLG or S-Log3, you should be able to apply the inverse OETF in your grading software and work in whatever color space you want (linear, ACEScct, etc.). If you want, you can just compress to rec.709 there! There's really no need to squash to rec.709 in the camera.

klodolph|prev|next|

Yeah. I’m not really a professional here but I would say sensitometry is something of an interest of mine. Take what I’m saying with the appropriate grain of salt.

Your camera has some sensor which records some range of values. The range is a lot wider than your monitor can reproduce and generally follows some curve up to a maximum value, where the highlights clip.

Log is something of a fantasy in that it represents an idealized, mathematical idea of what your camera does, but it does not really correspond to what the sensor does or what the monitor displays. You can convert your video to log, or from log, but the log data itself is only really useful as an intermediate step when post-processing. I don’t think you need to capture in log. I would just go for some “regular” wide like Canon’s Wide DR.

YMMV, I am a little old-school about these things and there is a lot of stuff that I prefer to do in-camera. (Be warned that while doing things in post gives you flexibility to undo your work and change your mind, there are a lot of things you can’t reasonably do in post… even things that seem basic like white balance are better to do with lighting or filters, and if you don’t understand the sensitometry then you can easily end up with footage that you can’t rescue in post.)
