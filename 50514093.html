I think it’s necessary to separate the art from the science.

A CCD captures light with a fixed exposure time and analog + digital gain. This gives you a raw, or in photographer speak, RAW frame. The issue being that the required bandwidth to store RAW frames of video is simply too high to be practical.

So, you need some form of compression. RED is a great example of seeing the problem and solving it. Now the issue is compressed RAW video is a patent minefield, and just recorded video equally so.

And so, we are resorting to tricks such as “log raw encoding”, or “just get the videographer to capture it right in the first place” just to get around the limitations of the storage format.

Honestly capturing images and displaying them accurately is such a mess that it’s a miracle that we have more than 8-bits of colour, and that was hard enough as it is. From a RAW Bayer frame to what you see on a monitor preview display is multiple layers of white balance, gain, equalisation, linear to gamma, and whatever your monitor decides to do with the digital signal it’s fed.

The point of shooting in a log format isn't to make the footage look a certain way; it's to reduce the degree to which quantization/clipping throws away information you want to have in post.

2) turn zebras on in your camera (they give you a visual warning of over/under exposed areas in your shot)

3) use the live histogram. If you have massive peaks somewhere, you need to adjust.

4) light properly. film looks like film, mostly because of the lighting allowing you to grade the way you do.

5) shoot a macbeth chart in your scene, that way you can use the auto tools to make a base grade.

Yeah. I’m not really a professional here but I would say sensitometry is something of an interest of mine. Take what I’m saying with the appropriate grain of salt.

Your camera has some sensor which records some range of values. The range is a lot wider than your monitor can reproduce and generally follows some curve up to a maximum value, where the highlights clip.

Log is something of a fantasy in that it represents an idealized, mathematical idea of what your camera does, but it does not really correspond to what the sensor does or what the monitor displays. You can convert your video to log, or from log, but the log data itself is only really useful as an intermediate step when post-processing. I don’t think you need to capture in log. It is a bit apples-to-oranges to compare camera stops to DisplayHDR.

Camera stops are about relative contrast ratios, whereas DisplayHDR is a luminance-first standard where the 600 and 1000 mandate absolute peak output nits [1].

If HDR were defined purely by contrast ratio, an OLED display peaking at just 100 nits would technically be "infinitely HDR" simply because its black level is zero.

[1] https://displayhdr.org/performance-criteria/
