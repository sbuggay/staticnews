how are you using D1 and Workers with this?

cldellow|root|parent|next|

Two small uses:

1. The app is a remix app, workers do server-side rendering, so that a vanilla `curl` shows the actual payload, not just a script tag that loads the app.

2. I'm a great big nerd, so I recently added the ability to post reviews/photos/GPX traces to your AT protocol PDS. The app has a Worker cron job that subscribes to Bluesky's Jetstream for events in its namespace, and indexes these into D1 in order to answer certain API calls like "get traces in this area" or "get reviews for this hike". So the app itself isn't the primary source of truth, a person's PDS (which they control) is. The app just maintains a cache/index that can be rebuilt.

yeah, basemaps are small enough to store and host cost effectively, but high res satellite imagery gets big and expensive fast as you go up in zoom level. I built my own satellite mosaic of North America for my app so I can offer users offline access to the tiles and had to stop at z17 because of projected storage requirements. high res global coverage can get you into the hundreds of TB range which is no longer in the "stick it on r2 and serve it with a worker" or "download and upload the entire dataset sequentially over your residential internet" territory

the fun/hard parts of the project weren't really scale dependent, although downloading source imagery and building the tile pyramids did take literally weeks to run. actually, the hardest part was getting demosaicing right, e.g. making the quilt seamless. filtering out cloudy and smokey/hazy tiles while picking alternatives from the same time of year so groundcover & crops are in a consistent growth stage so you don't end up with checkerboard patterns or cloud silhouettes randomly across your map. and there were other problems that came up more or less just because of how & when the satellite took the images that require exposure correction and more filtering

I also have a pelias index with 170M records for search and self-host valhalla for routing. the open source mapping ecosystem has really impressed me the more I learn with each project

perrygeo|parent|next|

You can use the pmtiles:// protocol in Maplibre GL, requires protomaps.js and a few lines of setup [1]

My experience with it is ... ok. It works. But going directly to cloud storage (s3 in my case) is unpredictable latency and neither the browser nor most servers really cache HTTP range requests granularly, so every tile is another high-latency round trip. Not worth it - I ended up putting Martin tile server in front of it.

I assume someone could write a protomaps adapter in WASM with some smart caching? Until then, Standard GET requests are just easier to cache and perform much better in practice. The downside being you have to run a server or statically generate files.

[1]- https://docs.protomaps.com/pmtiles/maplibre
