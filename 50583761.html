Things are pretty tough out there. In addition to empathy, let me try to offer some advice as someone observing industry trends from the outside for a while -- especially in the center of the AI insanity that is the Bay Area:

The buzzword everyone is looking for is "high-agency."

No, not those agents, but yes, those will be essential.

Basically employers want someone who will start something from scratch and take it to the finish line all by themselves. So my advice, which others have echoed here: Build something useful -- end-to-end -- from scratch -- with AI -– on your own -- that you can show off as soon as possible.

The key point is, "high agency" is by definition not something you can just put on your resume; It is something you show, not tell.

Yes, you need to do this even as you go through the absolute hell that is a job search. But trust me, this will a) help get a much better job, and b) help in the long run throughout and beyond your career. This will be the most valuable skill in the near future.

A few other thoughts:

0. Get a $100 Pro subscription. Either GPT or Claude, doesn't matter much, but you do want access to Fable or Astra. The volume and variety of things you can get done in that quota is astounding. Codex offers a free quota with Astra if you want to get a sense of things.

1. Target a domain you are already very familiar with. You can draw on your long experience as an engineer, or any personal interests you may have. Say a Math tutoring web app that your students could use. This will sharply cut down the time to MVP.

2. Being an end-to-end thing, this will necessarily be something multi-disciplinary. Think of it as doing a one-person startup, so you're going to be forced to do everythig: design, frontend, backend, infra, and maybe even GTM stuff like marketing. Yes, you may not know most of these things, and obviously won't be great at them, but with AI, everything will be good enough.

3. This conveniently also helps explain the gap in your resume. Nobody needs to know you did this in a few months vs 2 years unless they ask.

4. Even when using AI, keep your hands-on skill warm. Most companies still do old-school leetcode interviews.

5. As an extreme backup this might even become something you monetize yourself.

Hope this helps, and all the best!
