>There's no reason to think things have changed.

There are a few:

- A massive expansion in userbase for what previously would have been considered high-performance computing equipment. Now that no particular skills whatsoever are required to harness these to some productive end, demand is presumably seeing a step-change up.

- Concentration/consolidation in manufacturing, largely unchecked by the partial lack of national, and complete lack of international guardrails against collusion (price fixing, supply limiting, etc)

- The increase in capex required to manufacture commercially viable semiconductors in volume

- The practical cessation of semiconductor process technology advancement (compared to the '80s, '90s, and '00s, say)

Taken together, when you say you "give it five years at most", I suspect you are right when it comes to the rate of increase of price (of bits of RAM, flash, TFLOPs of compute, etc) but I do not think we will see continuing exponential decreases in cost - or if we do, that exponent will itself have been greatly diminished.

dotwaffle|prev|

Still cheaper than S3 / GCS / ABS!

$10 to $25/TB to store in one of those places, paid every month -- with the nerve to charge an additional $10/TB or so just to retrieve it at the lower end of that bracket. The "high" $27.50/TB in the article shows how much margin there is on these products. Sure, you might want to factor in replication and the server hardware, but you're still looking at paying it off in a few months with real hardware.

S3 is still the same pricing as 10 years ago, and the cost to egress that out to the internet is effectively over $27/Mbps/month -- what someone would have paid for internet transit about 20 years ago. You can buy that transit for $0.02 to $0.08/Mbps/month these days, meaning it's nearly 1000x what you'd pay elsewhere.

Let me put it another way: You can get a 100Gbps transit port for $2000 to $8000/month, capable of serving around 10,000,000 GB (assuming you fill that connection only to 33%), or you can get about 25,000 GB with the big three cloud providers. The hardware comes to about $50 to $70/TB, the rack space and power are relatively cheap.

Not just that, with your own build you can get an actual file system, not just blob storage.

Hell, you can even lease a dedicated server from Hetzner / OVH / whoever for about $2 to $3/TB/month if you didn't want to handle any of the hardware or networking portions of it.

As far as I can tell, the main reason there isn't any real competition on the data storage front is that if your compute is in one of those clouds, it can cost you literally the same to send those bytes to the remote storage as it would be to store those bytes for months using the cloud provider's offering.

I'm really looking forward to a resurgence in non-cloud providers again, it's long overdue.
