adrian_b|parent|next|

That theory is not really disproven, even if it was not the only reason for the huge accumulations of coal during the Carboniferous.

Coal has been always forming later, even today, but never in such quantities, because later it required much luckier conditions for the wood to not be decomposed by fungi.

The climate and the position of the continents during the Carboniferous must have contributed to the accumulation of coal, i.e. it is likely that a much greater percentage of the surface of the Earth was covered by some kind of swamps with warm climate, where plants were abundant and dead wood could submerge and be protected from decomposition, but this still does not appear to be able to explain the so great discrepancy in the amount of accumulated coal.

The most likely explanation is that all factors must have contributed, the conformation of the continents, the climate and the low abundance or absence of wood-digesting fungi, or perhaps there already were wood-digesting fungi, but their enzymes were not as efficient yet as those of later fungi, so they could decompose only a small part of the wood before it was buried in sediments.

adrian_b|root|parent|next|

I still believe that in the future synthetic hydrocarbons will retain an important role for the long-term storage of energy, for which batteries are inappropriate, due to self-discharging, and also for aerospace applications.

Producing synthetic hydrocarbons is already old technology, which was used at large scale before the decreasing price of oil made that non-competitive.

Today the most inefficient part remains the capture of the diluted CO2 from air and its conversion into concentrated CO2, which is easy to process further, e.g. by electrolytic methods that are more efficient than the hydrocarbon synthesizing methods used in the past.

If only a small fraction of the money that has been spent for much more frivolous purposes, like AI investment, had been directed to goals like improving the methods for concentrating CO2, we would have been in a much better situation today.
