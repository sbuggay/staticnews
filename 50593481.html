My wife is a lawyer. She has specialized in international law as it relates to financial institutions for decades. She gets really bent out of shape by people who’ve just read the law books and think that they know everything there is to know. These are the same type of people who are usually stupid enough to try to defend themselves in court. Her sister’s ex-husband is one of those.

My wife always says that the law is like a tripod. There is one leg that is formed by the laws as they are written down. There is a second leg that is formed by the precedents and case law that have developed around the written law. And then there is the third leg which is formed by the courts as they apply the law to individual cases.

And exactly how this works out is different by country and locality within that country. That’s why each state in the US has their own bar exam, and their own bar associations. That’s why most cities and other localities have their own bar associations, and they may have their own additional bar exams.

For example, in China, if all you did was read the laws as written in the books, you might think they have a very modern legal system, with lots of reasonable choices. But I am told that the law in China is actually whatever the judge says it is, and their judgement is final. So, you can ignore the other two legs of the tripod in China. And who appoints those judges? Who promotes those judges? To whom do those judges all kowtow?

In France, the official laws are written only in French. Anything you read in a different language is a translation, and not the actual law itself. There are other differences up in France as well.

So, having a system that lets you search the written laws and the precedents that have formed around them, that is a very useful thing. And it’s a very expensive service called LexisNexis. And it’s even more useful if it lets you look at the history of the way that a particular judge has ruled on specific cases. Which I believe that LexisNexis does.

If you can give me an API to the laws as they are written and nothing else, that might have some value for some people. But it’s hard to compete against LexisNexis or other legal research tools in this space.
