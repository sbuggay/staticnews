Piketty's "Capital and Ideology" starts to address this. It's pre-AI, but covers how societies dealt with this historically. Which is not very well. I'm still struggling through that book. Piketty shows that even the French Revolution didn't level out wealth distribution much. Beheading the aristocrats didn't help.

There have been fundamental changes over time. Piketty points out that societies before the industrial revolution were usually tripartite - nobility, priests, and peasants. He's done his homework, and reports that nobility plus priests consistently runs 2% to 4% of the population. Interestingly, there's almost always a priesthood, across a wide range of theologies.

But then came manufacturing, and the world changed. The "rise of the middle class" began. The world became more complicated and interlinked, and a middle class was needed to manage the details.

As computers progressed, the middle class was partly replaced by automation. AI is accelerating that, as we can see in new college graduate hiring statistics. Even before AI, about half of US college graduates were in jobs that didn't really need a college degree. That's getting worse.

The default direction of AI in the developed world seems to be a few people at the top, a lot of people at the bottom using their hands, and AIs in the middle. Maybe half the middle class goes. That's the problem of the next ten years.

Robotics isn't good enough yet to replace the operatives at the bottom. That may or may not change. Robots may be too expensive. That's probably the problem of the 2040s.

So what do we do now?
