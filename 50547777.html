There are two separate questions:

1. Is it possible for a human being, or even in general, to recognize wines by taste and smell, grouping them by grape type, region, year? The answer to this is clearly yes, based on many competitions on this topic.

2. Do significant parts of the public agree with true experts, those who score decently on point one, on the ultimately subjective category of wine quality and/or enjoyment? The answer here varies far more with the specific audience you select. But, since with 1 we've established clearly that a taste difference exists, it makes sense that there will be non-expert audiences who will develop a preference for certain tastes.

I personally still remember vividly when I first tasted a sip of a Barollo wine from a friend who was in love with it. I didn't have much experience with wine in general, and it felt to me as if I had stuffed my mouth full of cotton balls, the tanin was so overpwering that I could barely swallow that sip. But despite hating it, I could absolutely tell that wine apart from anything else I had tasted before.

It's also a very common effect with lots of both strong and subtle tastes. The general public tends to hate the taste of truffles, for example, especially on their first few tastes, but that doesn't mean that there isn't also a significant part of the public that loves them enough to justify the huge prices. Same effect happens for almost every food product - wines, whiskeys, cheeses, green/black/oolong teas, etc.
