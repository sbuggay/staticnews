GuB-42|root|parent|next|

It is technically frontier, if by "frontier" we mean the Pareto frontier.

Being at the Pareto frontier means that you can't get something better on one aspect without compromising on some other aspect. The trick is that it is easy to be at the Pareto frontier by having enough dimensions.

Here, we have 4: score, number of parameters, origin (US) and open-weight. You can get a better score or a lower number of parameters, but you have to compromise on at least one of the other aspects. That's a pretty weak frontier if you ask me.

Tade0|root|parent|next|

Experts are not typically moved between VRAM and system RAM during inference, as it would be too slow.

What happens instead is that the "dense" parts of the model like e.g. the router are put into VRAM and the remainder of VRAM is occupied by the KV cache and whatever experts still fit.

The rest - usually just the experts - reside in RAM, but it's bo big deal.

Additionally, some models utilise an n-gram table, of which only one row is read per token, so it can actually be stored on disk without issue. Example:

https://huggingface.co/ISTA-DASLab/Qwen3.8-Flash-Next-GSQ-RC...
