I swear, one of the weirdest things about big corporations is how people believe that all the important decisions affecting them are made in the boardroom.

They overwhelmingly aren't. We build our own hell: 99% of product decisions in places like Google or Microsoft come from your peers, line managers, and PMs. Your manager may talk to the top brass a couple of times in their life. Even big-ticket decisions like "we need to lay off 10%" don't really come from execs, they come from some mid-level guy in finance who says "our quarterly numbers are not looking too great" - and even more uncomfortably, from the implicit, cumulative pressure from employees who want the value of their stock grants to keep climbing or they will start interviewing elsewhere.

I have some recent insights from a company that executed an "everything must be AI" pivot and sat in some of the department strategy meetings. It was mostly line employees who came up with ideas such as "we need a dashboard to show token use across departments and give that to managers come performance review time". In the meantime, another AI maximalist faction was telling the execs (or outright lying, if you prefer) that the technology enabled them to 100x and it would be crazy not to mandate it.

Execs bear the blame insofar that they foster this sycophantic, over-eager corporate culture, but ultimately, the call is coming from inside the house.
