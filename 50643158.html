This is a good example of reductio ad absurdum.

There are probably texts which were lost in the destruction of Alexandria which can be fully rebuilt from other quotes, without needing to use any inference (in the older sense of the word). An example would be its complete inclusion in another book which analyzed the original text. Another example would be several surviving texts which each contain large selections of the original work, such that combining them would recreate the original work.

This seems like the low-hanging fruit of the challenge. This is "simply" a task of LLMs combing all of humanity's archives to pick juicy needles out of haystacks (some of these surviving works may be quite obscure).

Next we have situations where perhaps 50% of a lost text can be reproduced via the above method, and an idea of what the remaining 50% can be obtained through analysis of contemporaneous texts which reference the original (without quoting it, or perhaps by paraphrasing, or giving a nod to), as well as a historical review of the life of the lost text's author (including the point in their life in which they wrote the lost work, and perhaps surviving lost works by them which came after) and their contemporaries.

In these situations, an approximation of what they might have written can be constructed, which IMO would be quite valuable. Perhaps each section which is filled in by an LLM could have notes containing supporting citations, assumptions which were made, and reasoning for those assumptions.

I'd love to see this put to the test by taking a work for which 50% of its text could be reconstructed from other works which cite it, then training a model on a corpus which is completely absent of the 50% which "would be lost" if constructed from the referencing works alone, then ask the model to generate the original text to the best of its abilities, including research capabilities (but with a classifier sitting between the model and any research results which would prevent it from "cheating" accidentally or otherwise)

The work generated from such an attempt at restoration could then be compared to the complete original work to explore the hypothesis. It actually reminds me a lot of the process through which some older paintings have been restored, when the original is incomplete.
