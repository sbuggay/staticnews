gerdesj|parent|prev|next|

Quite. The public DNS is absolutely the wrong way to go about this. However there is nothing wrong with fiddling with DNS "internally" - that's a different issue.

It's all about truth and messaging.

DNS tells you where something is, no more and no less. When queried, it points to the map and says: "It's there, off you trot". DNS does not care what is there, it is just a guide.

DNS works over port 53/udp+tcp and could be fiddled with in transit. There is a variety of DNS called DNSSEC which, with care, can be trusted to be accurate (for your given value of accurate).

Messaging: As I said, port 53/udp+tcp could be fiddled with if DNSSEC is not in play. There is also DNS over HTTPS or DoH. Now the messaging channel is encrypted. At this point you have the same confidence in security as you do when looking at https web sites.

Now, you can obviously use DoH DNS from anywhere in the world and it will look like ordinary web traffic. Most browsers are already doing this.

Messing with DNS now in Europe is basically fiddling whilst Rome burns - bloody stupid and couterproductive. Hamstringing a service such as Quad9 is at best ridiculous.
