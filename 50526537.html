First of all, I really do think people got a bit carried away with the "ChatGPT, summarize this email" meme. AI really is a revolutionary technology, but low hanging fruit like note-taking, summarization, etc. are not even remotely a killer product.

Second, I also think that (outside of search), we'll look at the chatting interface of today's AI tools in 5 years and laugh at how antiquated they seem.

Finally, Obsidian (not to mention Apple Notes) is free. So to be competitive here, your value prop has to be an absolute home run. I'm never going to root against fellow entrepreneurs, but this just seems like a more pricy, maybe more stylish Obsidian (without all the plugin ecosystem? and you also need a browser extension?). Obviously, hindsight is 20-20, but if I were to release a productivity app today, I would do a few things:

1) Open Source it. Especially in the age of agents, people want to make sure that no shenanigans are being done with sensitive data, especially when it involves private notes.

2) Dual license it. Folks have done this since Red Hat, and I think dual licensing for personal & professional use (or have a revenue cutoff like Unreal or Unity) is the move.

3) Create a plugin ecosystem around it. I think the great thing about agents is that even non-technical folks can come up with stuff and vibe-code a plugin. Incentivize people to do this and build a community.

Aurornis|root|parent|next|

> or do founders become metaphorical indentured servants?

VCs invest in the company. They put money into the company and get equity in the company in return.

It’s not a loan to the founders. So don’t worry, they’re not indentured servants and everyone, including the VCs, knew this was a possible outcome. In VC it’s actually the most likely outcome because most companies don’t become big successes or even become valuable stable businesses.

What happens next: The remaining assets and liabilities will be settled up. Any remaining cash goes to the owners (equity), generally with some preference to paying back most recent investors first up (preference).

If the founders performed well and the investors believed they were doing good work that didn’t pan out due to market conditions or bad luck, they may be interested in investing with those founders again later. Or they may be sour and not want to talk to them. I’ve seen more of the former than the latter. Most VCs try to avoid getting bad reputations so they’ll make an effort to stay on good terms with founders.
