jmyeet|prev|next|

Cyberpunk is funny to me because it's like 1980s xenophobia forever preserved in amber.

What was happening in the 1980s? American manufacturing had begun crumbling while Japan (at least up until the late 1980s) was, as many Americans saw it, "taking over". Part of it the exotic aesthetics but really it's about the fears of Japanese economic and technological dominance.

I'd say this genre peaked with Snow Crash. But it became clear that the future isn't going to be like how VR was portrayed then. We've kind of moved on.

It's said that all art is political. Science fiction is particularly political. George Lucas has said the inspiration for the Resistance in Star Wars was the Viet Cong. The original trilogy is really anti-imperialist. And The Empire? That's the US. Part of the reason why the later trilogies felt so hollow (at least to me) is they lacked a deep and coherent theme.

Oh and I don't know what Mr Robot is doing on that list. I don't see it as remotely cyberpunk. What it is more than anything is anti-capitalist. Reading some other comments here it seems like people are mistaking dystopian for cyberpunk. Dystopian fiction existed long before cyberpunk. Like The Expanse is 100% not cyberpunk either.

cubefox|root|parent|next|

> I think to a certain extent the future caught up to cyberpunk which makes it no longer interesting.

A lot of our present would indeed have counted as a form of cyberpunk in the 80s and early 90s. Cyberpunk is one of the few sci-fi genres that had predicted the future "correctly", in a very loose sense, with the massive rise of information technology.

That is not to say that everything (or even most things) in cyberpunk stories has become reality. In fact, some scenarios that were once plausible futures are now no longer plausible. Particularly the fact that we are already on the brink of superintelligence is incompatible with most sci-fi futures where AI doesn't play a major role.
