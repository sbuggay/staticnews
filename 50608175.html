It's not the size of the screen, it's the Field of View (FoV) which is a function of viewing distance and screen size. This chart shows the FoVs recommended by SMPTE (Society of Motion Picture and Television Engineers), THX and 20th Century Fox: https://acousticfrontiers.com/blogs/articles/home-theater-vi.... RTings.com has one that's basically a blend of those: https://i.redd.it/qhis4tdy165h1.png.

There's no "correct" value because across that middle range, people have different preferences. As a home theater enthusiast and long-time video engineer (with an actual shiny Prime Time Emmy for Technical Achievement!) I've developed an opinion over many years across a lot of ideal screening rooms including Dolby's in SF, the old one on the 20th Century lot and more than a few multi-million dollar private rooms. I've learned my sweet spot for viewing cinematic content is right around 45 degrees. My wife prefers around 35. At home we have a custom theater with a 3-laser 4K front projector filling a 150" screen. Sitting in my custom recliner, the screen fills 45.2 degrees of my field of view.

Since I also enjoy some gaming and sports in our theater (at that size, GTA 6 on PS5 Pro is amazing), I wouldn't want the FoV any wider as I'd need to turn my head to track moving objects to the edges of the screen, so it really comes down to what your optimal peripheral vision zone is. Tracking objects like fans down front near the net of a Wimbledon tennis match isn't comfortable for most people.

Isn't the FOV in this case the FOV of your eyesight, not the FOV of your game character (what's displayed on the screen)? If you sit 2m away from a 17" screen, the screen will be a tiny portion of your FOV with the rest being the wall behind. If you sit an arm's length from a 100" TV your FOV will cover a fraction of the screen and have to turn your head around just to see different parts of the screen.

So the size of the screen needs to be balanced with the distance you sit away from it. In this light there is no TV too big, just unsuited for the viewing distance.

Merrill|prev|next|

The 480i format of NTSC television was engineered to be viewed from 6 or 7 times picture height away, given the normal visual acuity threshold of 1 arcminute.

The corresponding viewing distances for 1080 line and 2160 line formats are about 3 times and 1.5 times picture height. Consequentially, if you are sitting 3 times height or more away from the TV, you will not be able to see a difference in detail between a HD and 4K TV.

This would probably be true if both TVs were showing equivalent sources, for example:

- the same 4K stream, or

- native-resolution sources with full colour resolution (4:4:4) on both.

In practice, though:

- most video has half the colour resolution of its brightness resolution (4:2:0), so a 1080p source has only 540 lines of colour

- a 1080p TV usually gets a lower-bitrate stream from a streaming service, with more visible compression artifacts.
