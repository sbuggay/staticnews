somenameforme|parent|prev|next|

The wire fraud criminal code [1] dates back to 1952. It was originally intended to criminalize various little scams that were playing out on television advertising - bait and switch, exaggerations of quality of characteristics, and other such stuff [that's mostly expected in modern advertising ironically].

Because of this the legalese is super broad. It's now anything that 'obtains money or property by means of false or fraudulent pretenses or representations that is transmitted by wire, radio, or television communication." So basically any lie that can lead to the gain of anything material is wire fraud. For instance if you exaggerate your credentials on a resume that's sent by email, that's 100% wire fraud. You're probably not going to get prosecuted for that, but you could.

It's an absurdly broad law that needs to be reformed, but it won't be, precisely because it's now a super law. Anytime the government doesn't have an otherwise clear case against somebody - they can just charge them with wire fraud. And it comes with absolutely massive penalties including up to 20 years in prison.

---------

To be clear I'm not saying what happened here shouldn't be illegal, but that there should be a law like 'false billing' when a company charges for services that were not actually carried out. The current wire fraud law is just too broad and too powerful a weapon for the government to be expected to use responsibly in perpetuity.

[1] - https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/18/1343
