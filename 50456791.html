The short answer is: more and better data.

The long answer is: probably the same way any other classifier does. Take your ground truth probabilities and your predicted probabilities. The gap between those is called cross entropy, which is used to push the model towards the correct probabilities.

JacobAsmuth|root|parent|prev|next|

For a single prediction, sure. But the model doesn't make a single prediction and then stop existing. It makes an unbounded number of future predictions and under that assumption it is reasonable to expect the model to behave optimally under Bayes rule - which has a unique global minimizer at E[(Y-F(X))^2].

So counter to your claim that "It doesn't trivially imply that the model would learn sensible distributions under uncertainty" I think you need to consider what it means to optimally update a posterior under the global minimizer of the true conditional distribution.


sdl|root|parent|next|

The thing I had to wrap my head around is: For a "classic" classifier, the classes are fixed and the data distribution is p(x,y). The calibration is checked over this single distribution. Jev makes the tasks part of the input, so the data distribution is p(task,x,y) and calibration is averaged over that joint distribution. What follows is that while for classic classifiers, calibration brakes down if your x was out-of-distribution, it can now also break if your task is out of distribution. The big question now is: Can subtle differences in phrasing your task already lead to this? And the only way to verify calibration is having ground truth data to validate on. Also: Calibration over p(task, x, y) means calibrated on average across tasks. Even an in-distribution task can be over- or underconfident while the average looks fine.
