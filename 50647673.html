There's a lot to potentially discuss, but I'll try to train-of-thought some more relevant points into words.

Many creative arts aren't just about having an idea. Personally, I think ideas tend to be shockingly formless once you examine them up close, even ones that feel very concrete.

In things like visual arts, the "hard parts" aren't just throwaway. Every tiny stroke of a pencil, digital bush, etc. is a little decision that brings that longing formlessness into form. There are other important decisions during creation too; when you make a mistake do you incorporate it or throw it away? Each of these decisions may be easy or be difficult, but they're intentional.

While you you make those decisions, seeing the work materialize in front of you can cause the shape of the idea itself, your artistic vision, and even your tastes to change. The work is already influencing you and your idea before it's even finished.

When a person says something like, "I have an idea: a red building against a blue sky." they likely don't see every brick, or the shapes of the windows, or the way the strokes are laid. It's very easy for somebody else to show a picture of a red tower and infill most of the original idea their own. The original person's idea may be represented, but so are a lot of others. (Sometimes we even happily say, "Thanks. Here's $5 for filling that in.")

I feel that even very detailed prompts tend to only roughly approximate the level of intent expressed through the process of creating a piece. Intention isn't everything in art, but it's clearly an important something that's been a topic of discussion for a long time.

You also can't or won't prompt for certain things, so your end result will just never have those aspects. For example: An old discussion with a friend will never randomly cross your mind and causes you to accidentally lay a bunch of angry brush strokes in one place.

The formlessness of ideas also seems to make it relatively easy to give feedback. Again, real shape and decisions come when trying to turn that feedback to form.

If you just need a menu for your food truck or a picture of your grandchild with angel wings, maybe those rough prompts with a little refinement are serviceable. But, if your desire is to make some "art" and express your creativity or imagination, many potential viewers value things like intentionality (whether they realize it or not.) Outsourcing intent causes more weight to lean on an idea, so it better be a stellar idea, assuming it's even yours and not mostly the outsourcers--a question dangling over the viewer's mind.

Now compound that with generative AI making images a [mass-produced] commodity and causing viewers to be constantly bombarded with vague "ideas." How does an idea or a work stand out in that landscape? The level of intention becomes shorthand some viewers use to judge if they should pay attention to you. That makes it valuable to them and potentially an even more important differentiator than ever before.
