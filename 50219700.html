Zagitta|parent|next|

It's quite nuanced. In a nutshell the unlock just flips the 2 bits that halves how often the core can issue the specific instructions Nvidia locks to full rate on the professional and datacenter cards.

Obviously doing more compute requires more power but what it does is shift the card down on the frequency curve to keep the wattage in check. So you're right that at stock power limits it hits a cap where the full issuing rate isn't reached but it depends heavily on the exact workload how much juice is left.

The frequency curve also takes heat into account so in general you end up with a nice perf boost for a slightly hotter card.

I've been running this on my 2 3090s for a month now with no issues but they are also water cooled :-)

jacquesm|root|parent|next|

I put it together myself. It was a lot of work and I learned a lot of nasty lessons along the way. The short version of the recipe:

- get the largest PSU you can find (1500W at a minimum, more is better)

- get the ADT PLX Quad PCIe backplane. Run 4 thin (single slot) GPUs inside your main enclosure, stick the ADT interface board in between two of the thin boards (don't stack them close to each other or they won't be able to breathe).

- ensure you have as much or more RAM as you do VRAM

- Ensure your CPU has enough PCIe lanes to run at least five boards in x16 (four for the GPUs in the main enclosure, another for the PLX PCIe switch)

- In the BIOS set BAR1 to 'resizable' and > 4G decoding should be enabled.

- ditch the cables that come with the ADT board, they may work but there are much better ones out there and they're not expensive, this will save you days of troubleshooting

- Build your own enclosure rather than trying to adapt a standard one, it won't work anyway (the board pitch is wrong, as is the placement of the mounting holes).

- Put another four double or triple boards on the backplane.

- Add another 3x 140 mm fan to the front of the case so you get good airflow without the feeling of being next to a Harrier at take-off

- I used 3D printed parts to mount the PSU and the board as well as the fans (see below).

- Make sure the board is properly grounded to the enclosure

- If you're running 3090's or other cards that are P2P capable that were hobbled by NV then look at this https://github.com/aikitoria/open-gpu-kernel-modules driver module.

- Power on the ADT board, wait three seconds then power on the main machine. Enjoy your VRAM :)

If you have more questions please email me and I'll be happy to help out.
