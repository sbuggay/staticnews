Coral gets its color from algae called zooxanthellae, which it depends on for photosynthesis.

Bleaching is actually due to the coral expelling those algae. Bleached corals are not dead, and remarkably they can not only let the algae in again but can also select a different algae species to cultivate (changing colour in the process)!.

This is thought to be a deliberate survival strategy to optimize the species of algae under different conditions (Adaptive Bleaching Hypothesis).

Bleaching is caused by the same stressors (like high temperature) which kill coral, but bleaching itself is not death of the coral.

skew-aberration|root|parent|next|

I'm not an expert, but yes we can and we do.

And so does the coral!

If a species of algae cannot handle high temperatures without generating chemicals like hydrogen peroxide which trigger the bleaching, then that species is ejected sooner and more frequently during warmer months, creating selective pressure for species that can.

To survive these conditions without getting ejected, the algae need to essentially run their photosynthesis process more slowly. They produce less chlorophyll per cell and create sacrificial 'photoprotective carotenoid pigments' which act like sunscreen and dissipate that potential sunlight energy as useless heat (coral has a few tricks too fluorescent proteins).

So the coral is actually in a delicate balancing act, selecting the most aggressive/productive algae which won't also try to kill it during summer. Corals will learn to be more conservative in their selection and algae will get more conservative themselves. We want this process to happen quickly enough so that we don't lose coral coverage and diversity in the coming decades / centuries, but we can be confident that on longer timescales the coral will adapt, assuming we don't get runaway warming.

skew-aberration|root|parent|next|

Corals are killed en masse by other events, particularly cyclones. These have a much bigger affect on coral coverage, in the short term, than ocean temperatures / bleaching (which is transient and more localized).

Bleaching is not death of the coral, it is the expulsion of algae (typically in high temperatures). The coral can find a different species of algae, or the algae can return once temperatures drop.

There are corals living in regions of the ocean with different temperatures, so it is not that high temperature alone would kill all coral, but populations adapted to a given region (like north vs south GBR) are being affected by the speed of the changes.
