There’s a concept called the Deflection Dilemma (or the Sagan-Ostro Dilemma, after its creators), which argues that because we’d likely have enough advance warning to deflect a dangerous asteroid on an impact course, we should build the tech just-in-time rather than in advance. The argument is about dual-use risk: any technology capable of deflecting near-Earth objects (NEOs) away from Earth could also be used to redirect harmless ones toward us, and that humans are just irrational enough to actually do it.

I used to think the psychological part of the idea was weak, like why would anyone intentionally steer an asteroid into Earth? But the movie `Don’t Look Up` convinced me that a scenario where companies try to nudge asteroids closer to mine rare-earth minerals is plausible. Given how AI development is going, I think we should probably treat asteroid-deflection tech with the same caution we apply to nuclear or AI capabilities.
