I haven't used these extensions, but I like the direction of being able to extend Postgres by tailing its own internals (pg_squeeze approach) than triggers (pg_repack).

We (the SpiceDB developers) took a similar approach to solve a different problem a few years ago: implementing strict serializability in Postgres[0]. By hooking into the transactions layer themselves (pg_xact), we could layer on our own MVCC reusing the real Postgres transactions to compute visibility and total ordering. When you can just tail the Postgres internals, you don't even need to implement a Postgres extension, which can keep it portable across locked-down, managed Postgres offerings.

Do folks know of other clever implementations of behavior by "spying" on the Postgres internals?

Before long-running commands, open a screen [1] session. That way, if you get disconnected, the job keeps running, and you can reconnect to the screen session afterwards.

The man page is quite long, use "screen -R <appropriate name" to create the session or reconnect to it, and (IIRC) CTRL-A D to exit screen without closing the session.

[1] https://man7.org/linux/man-pages/man1/screen.1.html

QuentinM|prev|next|

I would have loved REPACK CONCURRENTLY when we were generating millions of dead tuples an hour on our trading database …

Sadly it doesn’t support partitions yet, which is typical when reaching sufficient scale that bloat management really matters. People often mention disk space when talking about dead tuples, but the real killer is what’s hidden under - bloat in the ctid & btree leaf linked lists make every lookup feel like table scans…!

Thankfully HOT (Heap-only Tuples) has saved us since. Virtually no dead tulles generated, and they get otherwise collected during regular ops like SELECT, amortizing cost over time, and preventing dangerous build up.
