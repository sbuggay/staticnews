Distributed post training over the internet is much easier than distributed pretraining. What we need is just agent traces, from everyone's daily use. Hugging Face already supports uploading agent traces, see https://huggingface.co/docs/hub/en/agent-traces

We've been developing DataClaw to help everyone collect agent traces, see https://github.com/peteromallet/dataclaw . There are other similar efforts, such as in https://old.reddit.com/r/LocalLLaMA/comments/1u795pb/donate_...

The raw CoT is encrypted for closed source models, but there are plenty of ways to decrypt them. Even without CoT content, the tool calls and the length of CoT are already good signals for post training.

In China we use 'transition stations' to access GPT and Claude. They collect GBs of plain text agent traces every day, and the data eventually belong to the open source community. My estimation is that there are already 10-100 TB of agent traces freely on the internet for everyone to download.

post training is both the future and the secret sauce.

The next iteration of AI assisted devs will not have all the devs use the same harness and model like most do now.

Instead, devs will build their own post trained models that are experts of their own domain, that have their secret sauce..

I imagine any dev would want most of those models to be something they can self host because of multiple reasons - one of them being that their employers or customers might not agree to use a cloud hosted or managed model. Plus, costs. And control.

Post training is in the future because we haven't yet reached a shared agreement on what that first "good enough" model will be like - and also because the current AI scape is in a state of flux where post training will have a lot of churn.
