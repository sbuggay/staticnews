The official WinHelp authoring tool was… WordPad. Or really, any RTF editor that supported the custom footnote glyphs that the WinHelp compiler turned into a .hlp file. But I would’ve been nervous about using WinWord in case of unwanted additional formatting.

For some reason this baffled me at the time, even though it’s really no different from DocBook to HTML conversion. Maybe it was inexperience, or maybe it was because HTML was its own well-known thing while .hlp was opaque, but I always thought that the footnotes etc. were semantically preserved to the final artifact, and I couldn’t wrap my head around how they became popups and hyperlinks.

Later, Compiled HTML Help arrived which was significantly less mysterious. Maybe because the input and output were still obviously HTML, packaged into a .chm with some metadata. Or maybe I’d just learned more.

The hyperlinked text file doco is based upon Pete Davis's 1993 articles in Schulman's "Undocumented Corner" in Dr Dobb's Journal. (https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.5555/178300.178317)

Pete Davis actually expanded and corrected those articles, and they are chapter 4 of xyr book, co-authored with Mike Wallace, Windows Undocumented File Formats, published by Taylor & Francis in 1997.

Unlike the txt file and the original DDJ articles, the book has diagrams. (-:
