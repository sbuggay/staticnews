Paracompact|parent|next|

> I recognize that there is probably no objective way to deductively prove it because of the Halting Problem etc.

There is. But it will only be reliable if every transitive dependency of your codebase, all the way down to the hardware level, has been formally verified. Failing that, you have to rely on opaque contracts at the boundaries, and those are just promises that may be mistaken.

The Halting problem, Rice's theorem, etc. only apply if your goal is to automatically and completely deduce properties or their violations for arbitrary programs. If your goal is simply to prove security and correctness properties for well-understood classes of programs in industry, then there are no fundamental obstructions.

Paracompact|root|parent|next|

The bar for Turing-complete is very low. The halting problem is to programming as Godel's incompleteness theorem is to metamathematics. Yet no one is seriously suggesting that we downgrade to axiomatic systems incapable of expressing Peano arithmetic, nor that our current axiomatic systems are impractical for proving theorems.

We cannot automatically and completely verify nontrivial semantic properties of arbitrary programs written in a Turing-complete language (Rice's theorem), but as long as we as humans can intuit correctness arguments for our programs, then formal methods will apply to it. Of course, declarative rather than imperative programming semantics tend to result in simpler correctness arguments, sidestepping reliance on global state and convoluted loop invariants.

rurcliped|prev|next|

The blog is misleading because it tries to characterize Project Glasswing's effectiveness through use of a tangentially related data source. A Project Glasswing vulnerability discovery most often occurs when a Glasswing partner points a Mythos-class model at its own, often proprietary, software. The data source (aka "ledger") is only for the Anthropic coordinated vulnerability disclosure program for open source software: in other words, Anthropic itself pointing a Mythos-class model at an OSS package, and then notifying the maintainer afterward that it had done this, and had findings to share. This OSS work is certainly useful, but the number of fixes reflects - in part - limited time availability by volunteer OSS maintainers, and Anthropic sometimes working without knowledge of the OSS project's threat model (the threat model is explicitly requested in the newer oss-scanner effort). But if you're an interesting target, your defense can't be based on the ability to find out about every single software defect ahead of the time. You need layers. And if you have that, the importance of proactively acquiring every 0-day is diminished.
