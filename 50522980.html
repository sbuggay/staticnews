Hi! I'm Phillip Compeau, I teach in Carnegie Mellon's Griffin School of Computer Science, and I run CS Learn, which launched today.

We're releasing full semesters of lectures from current CMU computer science courses across a variety of topics, with one course premiering each day this week. More courses come in January.

Yes, MIT got here 25 years ago :). What I'm excited about is how we're doing it: a few of our students built tools that automate a lot of the production work, so a small team can get a course out quickly and still have people review every lecture.

We'll have lots more coming across a wide array of subjects. I'd love to know if there are topics you would like to see us cover!

kergonath|root|parent|next|

From my experience using LLMs and supervising PhD students, the LLM is too focused on solving a task rather than educating or opening paths. It’s great if you have a specific question to ask, even at quite high level, it can explain you group theory or quantum chemistry quite well if you ask the right questions. The problem is that if students knew the right questions they would not be students. LLMs can get stuck explaining a topic without realising that it would be useful or interesting to branch out to something else. They can give you all the pros and cons of a given simulation technique without even trying to tell which of those actually matter in a specific case. They tell you if you ask, but you have to know what to ask.

LLMs are not great at open-ended discussions and don’t benefit of experience in the same way humans do. They are better to solve problems than to explore ideas. They keep getting better, though.
