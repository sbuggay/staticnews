jbstack|root|parent|next|

Repeating a sentence twice in a row during an explanation, is not spaced repetition at all. Spaced repetition means testing recall at increasing intervals, usually starting 1 day apart.

"Paris is the capital of France. Paris is the capital of France" -> not spaced repetition.

"Paris is the capital of France. [one day later]. What is the capital of France? [three days later] What is the capital of France?" -> spaced repetition.

And in fact, it's this plus practice where the real value lies in learning and memorising. The only purpose of an explanation from a teacher is to help you understand the concept. Generally once you understand it once, you don't need to understand it again - unless you forget, and that's where the practice / spaced repetition comes in. That's why I prefer the teaching part to be as efficient as possible, so I can focus my limited time on the parts that will make me internalise it.

My understanding is that the idea behind Khanacademy is to switch the classroom -- learn a topic as homework; do the questions in the classroom. That way the teacher can assess (and help) the students.

The other idea is that a student only advances to the next topic once they've mastered the current topic (e.g. by getting 4/5 questions right). The theory behind this is that if you don't understand a concept/topic then it is harder to understand topics that depend on that.

This does mean that students will be at different topics/stages. But because of the structure of the teaching a teacher isn't moving on to a new topic leaving students to fall further and further behind.

This resonates so much with me. I am the co-founder of a microschool and also a neuroscientist. And these two have intersected so much in fascinating ways. Four years in, the big lesson for us is that for learning to make sense it should have the following progression: meaning, motivation, mechanics, measurement.

Conventional schooling, and everything designed to help with conventional schooling like Khanmigo give in to this systemic gravitational pull of measurement (we must know what they are learning). And from that ensues your standardized curriculum, measuring the visible and easy, because that's easier than something like "mathematical thinking", and the focus on mechanics (multiplication tables, fractions, chemical names, whatever). Chatbots, the way they are set up now, are doomed to fail because the entry point is mechanics. "How do I solve this particular quadratic equation?"
