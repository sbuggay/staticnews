Just Josh Tech on YouTube reviews all manner of laptops thoroughly, and I generally like their style of coverage.

My general recollection is that battery life is good, graphics performance is weak, Windows on ARM is nearly there but there are still niches where you need to watch out for performance hits/compatibility.

Qualcomm laptops have a major strength in having frequent discounts and sales.

I think Qualcomm’s main issue is that Intel mostly caught up with ARM with the Core Ultra Series 3. Better GPU, multi-core, and even better battery life than the M5, with the only weakness being single core performance (which still isn’t bad).

The main issue with competing with Apple is that once you bump up to the Pro and Max variants, they have a very unique offering that nobody else really has. You could buy something like a Zephyrus G14 and try to brute force the GPU performance to have something somewhat comparable (and it’s certainly a better choice for gaming), but that CPU performance is just not possible to beat in any other similar laptop.

The other issue with competing with Apple is that so far the laptops that have hit the market with the Core Ultra X7 variants are a bit too expensive to compete with a MacBook Air. The lower model Lenovo X9 15p gets somewhat close by having the sleeper build 338H with 10 GPU cores, but it’s still a $2000 computer. It’s equivalently priced to a MacBook Air since it comes with 32GB/1TB, but perhaps they need a 16GB model to compete in the same realm as the base model MacBook Air.

Still, in the realm of base model laptop processors, Apple is looking more beatable than they ever have been since 2020 when M1 hit the scene. We even see this with the MacBook Neo where HP and Dell easily leapfrogged the value proposition of that laptop within a year, offering faster computers with backlit keyboards and longer battery life at the same price, and in addition to that they offer 16GB upgrades of those models.
